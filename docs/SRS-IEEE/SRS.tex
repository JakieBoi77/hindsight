\section{Introduction} 
% ==============================================================

This document is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for \progname{}, a mobile game
that prepares children aged 3 to 7 years for paediatric ophthalmology appointments.
This document defines the expected behaviour, scope, and requirements of \progname{},
establishing a basis for implementation, verification, and validation by describing what the system
is expected to accomplish and the conditions under which it will be used.

This document follows the IEEE SRS template provided in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate},
with guidance from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 \cite{ISOIECIEEE291482018} for requirements engineering and
ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 \cite{ISOIECIEEE152882023} for systems life-cycle management. The team made
modifications to the original template to better suit the project's needs. In Section~\ref{non-func-req}
(Non-Functional Requirements), the requirements are organized according to the Volere template provided
in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate}. The AI and Machine Learning Requirements section was
also removed, as it is not applicable to the project.

\subsection{Purpose}
\label{purpose}

The purpose of \progname{} is to reduce anxiety and uncertainty in children before paediatric
ophthalmology appointments by providing accessible, age-appropriate preparation for common eye
examination procedures. \progname{} aims to improve children's preparedness and willingness to participate while helping
parents and healthcare professionals prepare them for appointments. Intended outcomes include
reduced anxiety, improved cooperation, sustained engagement across the target age range,
more effective preparation at home, and greater clinical efficiency. The application complements,
rather than replaces, professional guidance and medical examinations.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec_Scope}

\progname{} is a mobile application intended to help children aged 3 to 7 years become familiar
with common paediatric ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment. The application
will present preparation activities as age-appropriate interactive games that allow children to
practice actions and learn what they may experience during an eye examination.

The primary users are children preparing for ophthalmology appointments, with parents or guardians
supporting their use of the application. Ophthalmologists and child-life specialists are secondary
stakeholders who may provide clinical and developmental guidance during development and may use
the application as part of a broader preparation process.

The application will provide interactive activities corresponding to common ophthalmology
procedures, provide feedback during activities, track completion of preparation activities, and
allow users to repeat activities when needed. The application will be designed to support
preparation before an appointment, including preparation in a home environment.

The expected benefits are increased familiarity with ophthalmology procedures, reduced uncertainty
and anxiety, improved cooperation during examinations, sustained engagement, and potentially
shorter or less difficult clinical appointments.

\progname{} will not diagnose medical conditions, provide medical advice, replace an ophthalmologist,
or determine whether a child requires a particular medical procedure. The application will not
replace an actual clinical examination. Medical decisions will remain the responsibility of qualified
healthcare professionals.

The initial system will focus on preparation for common ophthalmology procedures and will not
attempt to cover every possible procedure or clinical situation. Features identified as stretch
goals, such as augmented reality simulations and personalized preparation based on appointment
information, are outside the minimum required scope of the initial system.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
\label{problem-statement}

Paediatric ophthalmology appointments can be stressful and unfamiliar for children.
Procedures such as vision testing, eye drops, and slit-lamp examinations may cause anxiety, particularly when children do not know what to expect.
This anxiety can make it difficult to cooperate during examinations, potentially affecting the quality and completeness of clinical assessments.
For example, a study of 298 children undergoing cycloplegic eye examinations found that 26\% experienced significant distress before receiving eye drops,
while uncooperative children were also more anxious and required more time to complete the examination \cite{ChiaEtAl2015}.

Children and their families therefore need adequate preparation for common ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment.
Without appropriate preparation, unfamiliar clinical experiences may contribute to increased anxiety, reduced cooperation, and longer or more difficult appointments.
This project addresses the need for an accessible, child-friendly way to help children become more familiar and comfortable with the paediatric ophthalmology experience.

\subsection{Product Vision}
\label{sec_Product_Vision}

\progname{} is an interactive mobile application designed to help
children aged 3--7 feel more comfortable and prepared for paediatric
ophthalmology appointments. Through age appropriate games and activities,
the application introduces children to common procedures, including
vision testing, eye drop administration, and slit lamp examinations.
By allowing children and their families to become familiar with these
procedures before their appointments, \progname{} aims to reduce
anxiety, improve cooperation during examinations, and support a
more efficient clinical experience.

The primary goals of \progname{} are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduce Children's Anxiety:}
    Help children understand what to expect during common ophthalmology
    procedures, reducing anxiety associated with unfamiliar clinical
    experiences.

    \item \textbf{Improve Children's Cooperation During Examinations:}
    Prepare children to participate more cooperatively in clinical
    examinations, reducing interruptions and repeated attempts
    during procedures.

    \item \textbf{Maintain Engagement Across Age Groups:}
    Provide engaging and age appropriate activities that encourage
    children across the target age range to actively participate
    in their preparation.

    \item \textbf{Enable Effective At-Home Preparation:}
    Allow children and their families to independently prepare
    for upcoming ophthalmology appointments through accessible
    and understandable activities.

    \item \textbf{Improve Clinical Efficiency:}
    Support more efficient ophthalmology examinations by improving
    children's preparedness and reducing difficulties associated
    with completing clinical procedures.
\end{itemize}

These goals establish the intended outcomes of \progname{} and provide
the foundation for the functional and non-functional requirements
defined in this document.

The touch input and audio-visual output of the child and caregiver physically
pass through the device. The figure shows the two users separately because they
provide different inputs and need different outputs. The responsibilities of
each entity are listed below in terms of its inputs and outputs.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Child:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Provides touch input to choose a character, select levels, play the
    procedure games and spend coins on cosmetic accessories.
  \item Receives the preparation content, feedback and rewards through
    pictures, animation and narration, without needing to read.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Caregiver:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Installs \progname{} and acknowledges the medical notice before first
    use.
  \item Enters local preferences, such as the child's optional nickname and age
    group.
  \item Confirms actions that cannot be undone, such as resetting saved data.
  \item Reads the procedure guide and the notice.
  \item Supervises younger children as needed.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Device operating system and hardware:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Delivers touch events and life-cycle events, such as the application
    being sent to the background, closed or resumed.
  \item Displays images and plays audio at the volume the caregiver sets.
  \item Stores and returns the application's saved data in storage that only
    the application can read (\nfrref{NFR_DataProtection}).
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Clinical and child-life reviewers:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Approve the procedure content before each release. This includes the
    steps, equipment and sensations shown.
  \item Confirm that the content is suitable for children aged 3 to 7
    (\nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe}).
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{App store:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Delivers the signed installation package and later updates
    (\nfrref{NFR_Integrity}, \conref{C_StoreCompliance}). The package contains
    all procedure content.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{\progname{}:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Presents the approved procedure games and caregiver information without
    a network connection (\conref{C_OfflineOperation}).
  \item Accepts only the touch inputs expected on the current screen.
  \item Ignores repeated or accidental touches that would skip procedure
    information.
  \item Records progress, rewards, character choices and preferences.
  \item Stores no identifying information or clinical records
    (\conref{C_PatientPrivacy}).
  \item Checks saved data when it is loaded. If the data is missing or invalid,
    the application recovers without blocking access to any level.
  \item Stops all audio and animation of a level when the child exits it.
  \item Keeps the child's progress when the application is interrupted.
  \item Never transmits data off the device (\nfrref{NFR_Privacy}).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Context of use:} \progname{} is used for health education. Families
use it at home before an appointment or in a clinic waiting room. It is not
mission-critical. It is also not safety-critical in the sense of controlling
equipment or informing clinical decisions. It makes no diagnostic or treatment
claim (\nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}).

However, its users are young children in a medical setting. Some failures can
increase a child's anxiety or reduce cooperation at the appointment, which is
the opposite of the application's purpose. Examples are inaccurate or
frightening content, hidden sensations, punishing feedback, privacy failures,
and a caregiver treating the game as medical advice. The Hazard Analysis
document analyses these hazards. The resulting safety and security requirements
are included in this SRS. Requirements that address these hazards receive a
fuller rationale than other requirements.

\subsection{System Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \progname{} shall operate on consumer smartphones and tablets running
    iOS or Android.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_MobilePlatforms})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Patient families and clinics may use mobile devices running
        either operating system. Supporting both enables preparation
        at home and in clinic without assuming a particular platform.
    \end{itemize}

    \item Third-party assets and libraries included in \progname{} shall have
    licenses allowing their use and redistribution within the application.
    Required copyright notices, licenses, and attributions shall be retained.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_ApplicationLicensing})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} The application's source code is licensed under the
        MIT license, while third-party materials have their own licensing.
        These conditions must be satisfied when the application is released.
    \end{itemize}

    \item Third-party materials present in the \progname{} GitHub
    repository shall have licenses allowing redistribution in the form committed to the repository.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_RepositoryLicensing})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Permission to distribute an asset in
        an application does not necessarily mean redistribution of its raw
        files in the project repository is permitted. Repository contents must satisfy
        the applicable licensing conditions (separately from the compiled application).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall be built using a game engine with native support
    for cross-platform deployment to iOS and Android.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_CrossPlatformEngine})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Native deployment support satisfies \conref{C_MobilePlatforms}. The team has
        currently selected the Unity game engine, but the selected engine may
        change provided that it satisfies this constraint.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall provide all included preparation modules and their
    required assets without internet connection after installation.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_OfflineOperation})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Families must be able to prepare at home and
        in clinic, where connectivity may be poor or unavailable.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall operate as a standalone local application and shall
    not initiate network communications during operation, including
    through included third-party libraries.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_NoNetworkCommunications})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require external
        services or data transit. Prohibiting network
        communications also prevents included libraries from transmitting usage
        data. Downloading and updating the application through its distribution
        channels are outside normal application operation (i.e., a patient progressing through the application's
        preparation modules).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \progname{} shall not request, collect, or store identifying
    information or clinical records, and shall not integrate with clinic
    information systems.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_PatientPrivacy})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require patient
        records. Limiting application data to local gameplay, like character selection and scores,
        supports preparation without requiring identifying information.
    \end{itemize}

    \item The iOS and Android releases of \progname{} shall comply with the Apple App Store and Google Play submission
    requirements, respectively, including applicable policies for applications meant for children, at time of submission.
    (C.\refstepcounter{connum}\theconnum\label{C_StoreCompliance})
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rationale:} These marketplaces are the planned distribution
        channels through which families and clinics will install the application. Compliance
        with their submission requirements is necessary for distribution to the target users.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Use Cases}

The following use cases describe how children and caregivers use
\progname{} to explore ophthalmology preparation games through a
journey of levels. All procedure game levels remain accessible regardless
of whether earlier levels have been completed. Children may choose a
character, earn journey points, receive in game coins or cosmetic items,
and use owned items to customize their character. The personal scoreboard
shows the child's own progress, not a ranking against other children.
The reward amounts, character designs, available accessories, and specific
gameplay interactions described below are initial design proposals that
will be refined with the project team and clinical supervisors.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_FirstUse}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_FirstUse}: Complete Initial Application Setup}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Caregiver

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The application is installed on a supported device.
    \item The application is being opened for the first time, and no
    caregiver acknowledgment has been recorded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The caregiver opens \progname{}.
    \item The system displays a welcome screen explaining that the
    application helps children become familiar with eye examinations
    through interactive games.
    \item The system displays a notice stating that the application is
    an educational preparation aid and not a substitute for professional
    medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
    \item The caregiver reads the notice and selects the acknowledgment
    control.
    \item The system records the acknowledgment locally.
    \item The system displays an introduction to the journey, explaining
    that all procedure game levels are available and that children can
    earn points and cosmetic rewards as they play.
    \item The caregiver continues to the home screen, where the child
    can choose a character and start exploring the journey.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Notice Not Acknowledged:} If the caregiver does not
    acknowledge the notice, the system does not complete initial setup.
    \item \textbf{Setup Interrupted:} If the application closes before
    acknowledgment is saved, the system displays the notice again on
    the next launch.
    \item \textbf{Returning User:} If acknowledgment was previously
    recorded, the system opens the home screen without repeating setup.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The caregiver has been informed of the application's purpose
    and limitations.
    \item Acknowledgment is recorded locally, and the child can access
    the journey without creating an account.
\end{itemize}

Child

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_VisionGame}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_VisionGame}: Play the Vision Testing Level}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The child has selected Vision Testing on the journey map.
    \item The level's introduction is displayed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The system plays a child friendly introduction showing
    how a clinician may ask a child to identify symbols during
    a vision test.
    \item The system explains the matching activity using narration
    and visual examples rather than requiring reading.
    \item The system displays a reference symbol and several
    possible matching symbols.
    \item The child selects the matching symbol or selects skip.
    \item The system gives encouraging feedback on the selection
    or acknowledges the skipped round without penalty.
    \item Steps 3--5 repeat with different symbols until every
    required matching round has been attempted or skipped.
    \item The system displays the end of level screen and records
    the Vision Testing level as Completed.
    \item The system initiates the first completion reward process
    described in \ucref{UC_CompleteGame}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incorrect Match:} The system offers another attempt
    or lets the child skip to the next symbol.
    \item \textbf{Skip Round:} The system advances to the next symbol
    without requiring a correct response.
    \item \textbf{Exit Early:} The child selects home before the final
    round, the system returns to the journey map and retains an
    In Progress status rather than marking the level Completed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If the end of level screen was reached, the level is
    marked Completed even when some rounds were skipped.
    \item The game does not measure visual acuity or provide
    clinical results.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_EyeDropGame}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_EyeDropGame}: Play the Eye-Drop Preparation Level}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The child has selected Eye-Drop Preparation on the journey map.
    \item The level's introduction is displayed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The system introduces a virtual patient character and
    explains that a clinician may place drops in a child's eyes.
    \item The system illustrates a preparation action, such as having
    the virtual patient tip their head back.
    \item The child uses the on-screen controls to help the virtual
    patient perform the demonstrated action, or selects skip.
    \item The system responds with encouraging feedback or
    acknowledges the skipped action.
    \item Steps 2--4 repeat for the remaining preparation actions.
    \item The system presents an animation showing a clinical
    professional administering eye drops to the virtual patient.
    \item The child watches the animation or uses skip to bypass
    it if uncomfortable.
    \item The system explains that the child may tell the clinician
    if they feel worried or uncomfortable during an examination.
    \item The system marks the Eye-Drop Preparation level Completed
    and opens its end of level reward screen.
    \item The system initiates the first completion reward process
    described in \ucref{UC_CompleteGame}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Incorrect Action:} The system shows a visual hint
    and allows a retry or skip.
    \item \textbf{Uncomfortable with Animation:} The child skips the
    administration animation and continues to the concluding message.
    \item \textbf{Exit Early:} The child selects home, the game
    stops and remains In Progress rather than Completed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If the child reaches the ending, the level is Completed
    regardless of skipped individual steps.
    \item The application does not instruct the child to administer
    real medication or carry out the procedure independently.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}: Play the Slit-Lamp Preparation Level}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The child has selected Slit-Lamp Preparation on the journey map.
    \item The level's introduction is displayed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The system introduces a simulated examination room and

    \item The system demonstrates how a virtual patient places

    \item Steps 2--4 repeat for the forehead rest and other

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_ExitGame}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_ExitGame}: Exit a Level Before Completion}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The child is playing a procedure game level and has not
    reached its ending.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item During a Vision Testing matching round, the child decides
    to stop playing.
    \item The child selects the visible home control.
    \item The system stops the matching interaction, its narration,
    animations, and game specific input processing.
    \item The system records the Vision Testing level as In Progress
    unless it had previously been completed.
    \item The system returns the child to the journey map.
    \item The system displays the Vision Testing level with its
    current status while leaving every other level accessible.
    \item The child's existing journey points, coin balance, and
    owned character accessories remain unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Other Level:} The same exit behaviour applies while
    playing the Eye-Drop or Slit-Lamp Preparation level.
    \item \textbf{Animation Playing:} If the child selects home during
    an animation, the system stops the animation and audio before
    displaying the journey map.
    \item \textbf{Replay:} When the child reselects an interrupted
    level, it restarts at its introduction, the level is not
    falsely recorded as Completed.
    \item \textbf{Device Interruption:} If the application is closed
    during a level, reopening it restores access to the journey
    and preserves previously saved progress and rewards.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The interrupted level is no longer displayed or processing
    interactions.
    \item The child's saved rewards and other completed levels
    remain unchanged.
    \item No first completion reward is issued for the interruption.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_CompleteGame}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_CompleteGame}: Receive a Level Completion Reward}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The child has reached the ending of a procedure game level
    after attempting or skipping its exercises.
    \item The system has a predefined first completion reward for
    the level.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child reaches the end of the Vision Testing level.
    \item The system records the level as Completed and checks
    whether its first completion reward was previously claimed.
    \item Because the reward is unclaimed, the system adds the
    level's journey points to the child's personal total.
    \item The system awards the Vision Testing level's predefined
    quantity of in game coins and adds them to the child's coin balance.
    \item The system displays a reward screen showing the journey
    points earned, the coins received, and the updated coin balance.
    \item The child selects continue.
    \item The system returns to the journey map and displays the
    completed level with its reward recorded.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct Cosmetic Reward:} If the level's reward is
    a cosmetic item rather than coins (for example, a star shaped
    accessory for the Eye-Drop level), the system adds that item
    directly to the child's inventory, displays its preview, and
    offers a way to equip it. No coins are added for that reward.
    \item \textbf{Skipped Exercises:} If the child reached the ending
    after skipping some exercises, the system still awards the
    predefined participation based reward.
    \item \textbf{Replay Completed Level:} If the child completes an
    already rewarded level again, the system congratulates the child
    but does not award duplicate points, coins, or cosmetic items.
    \item \textbf{Exit Before Ending:} If the child exits before the
    ending, the system does not issue the first completion reward.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The first completion reward is recorded only once for
    the level.
    \item Journey points and coin balance or owned cosmetic items
    are updated locally as appropriate.
    \item Rewards represent participation, not visual ability
    or clinical readiness.
\end{itemize}

Child

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_PurchaseAccessory}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory}: Purchase a Character Accessory with Coins}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A character is selected and the application is open.
    \item The child has earned enough in game coins for at least
    one accessory in the cosmetic shop.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child selects the character shop from the journey map.
    \item The system displays cosmetic accessories, such as hats
    and glasses, with visual previews and prices in in game coins.
    \item The child selects an unowned hat.
    \item The system displays the hat on the selected character,
    its coin price, and the child's current coin balance.
    \item The child selects the purchase control.
    \item The system verifies that the child has enough coins and
    that the selected hat has not already been purchased.
    \item The system subtracts the hat's coin price from the
    spendable coin balance and adds the hat to the child's inventory.
    \item The system displays a purchase confirmation and offers
    an option to equip the new hat.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Insufficient Coins:} If the coin balance is too low,
    the system displays the shortage and does not deduct coins
    or add the item to the inventory.
    \item \textbf{Item Already Owned:} If the child selects an owned
    item, the system identifies it as owned and offers to equip
    it rather than charging coins again.
    \item \textbf{Cancel Purchase:} If the child returns to the shop
    without confirming, no coins or inventory items change.
    \item \textbf{Direct Reward Item:} An accessory received directly
    from a completed level appears as owned and is not purchased again.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The purchased accessory is available in the child's inventory.
    \item The coin balance decreases by the stated price, but total
    journey points and completed levels do not change.
    \item No real money payment, account, or Internet connection is used.
\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_EquipAccessory}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_EquipAccessory}: Customize a Character with an Owned Accessory}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A character is selected.
    \item At least one cosmetic accessory has been purchased
    with coins or received directly as a level reward.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child selects the character customization button.
    \item The system displays the current character and its
    equipped accessories.
    \item The child opens the owned accessories collection.
    \item The system shows a hat previously earned as a direct
    level reward alongside other owned items.
    \item The child selects the hat.
    \item The system previews the hat on the character.
    \item The child selects equip.
    \item The system updates the character's appearance and saves

\textbf{Alternative Flows}

    \item \textbf{Replace Accessory:} If another item occupies the
    same cosmetic slot, the system replaces the displayed item
    without deleting either item from the inventory.
    \item \textbf{Locked Accessory:} If the child selects an item not
    owned, the system does not equip it and shows how it can be

\section{Data Requirements}
\label{sec_Data_Requirements}

This section describes the information managed by \progname{}
to support journey based progression, character customization,
game completion tracking, rewards, and a personal scoreboard.
The application does not require patient accounts or
integration with clinical information systems. All user
data is maintained locally on the user's device.

Each ophthalmology procedure is represented by one
preparation game, which acts as a level in the child's
journey. Games may contain multiple smaller interactions
or rounds, but these are treated as part of the game
rather than as separate data entities.

All levels remain accessible regardless of whether previous
levels have been completed. Children can earn journey points,
receive in game coins or cosmetic accessories, and use
their rewards to customize a selected character.

Journey points accumulate across completed games and are
displayed on the personal scoreboard. In game coins are
tracked separately and may be spent on cosmetic accessories.
Rewards are granted upon the first completion of each game,
including when individual exercises have been skipped.


\subsection{Major Data Entities}

The system manages the following major data entities:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Procedure Game:}
    Represents a game designed to familiarize children
    with a particular ophthalmology procedure, such as
    vision testing, eye-drop administration, or slit-lamp
    examinations. Each procedure has one corresponding
    game that represents a level in the child's journey.
    A game may contain multiple interactive exercises
    or rounds, and its content may vary according to
    the child's age group.

    \item \textbf{Character:}
    Represents a character available for the child to
    select and use throughout the preparation journey.
    Each character has a unique identifier, a display
    name, and a visual representation.

    \item \textbf{Cosmetic Accessory:}
    Represents a virtual item, such as a hat or glasses,
    used to customize a character's appearance.
    Accessories may be purchased using in game coins
    or received directly as game completion rewards.
    Cosmetic accessories do not affect gameplay
    performance or clinical preparation activities.

    \item \textbf{Character Inventory:}
    Represents the cosmetic accessories owned by the
    child and identifies which accessories are currently
    equipped on the selected character. Owned items
    remain available when the child changes characters.

    \item \textbf{Reward Definition:}
    Represents the predefined reward associated with
    the first completion of a procedure game.
    Each reward includes journey points and either
    in-game coins or a cosmetic accessory.
    Rewards are based on game completion rather
    than the accuracy of individual responses.

    \item \textbf{Local Preferences:}
    Represents locally stored application settings,
    including the child's optional nickname, selected
    character, age group, display or accessibility
    preferences, and caregiver acknowledgment of
    the application's educational purpose.
    These settings do not require a patient account.

    \item \textbf{Game Progress:}
    Represents the completion status, journey points
    earned, and reward claim status for each preparation
    game. Progress is tracked at the game level rather
    than for individual rounds or exercises.
    This information reflects participation in the
    application rather than clinical performance
    or assessment results.

    \item \textbf{Journey Progress:}
    Represents the child's overall progression across
    all preparation games, including accumulated journey
    points, completed levels, and the current in game
    coin balance. Total journey points and completed
    levels are calculated from Game Progress records,
    while the spendable coin balance is maintained
    locally and updated when coins are earned or spent.

\end{itemize}

The relationship between these entities is as follows:
each Procedure Game represents one ophthalmology procedure
and corresponds to one level in the journey. Each game has
an associated Reward Definition and a Game Progress record.

Local Preferences identifies the character selected by the
child, while Character Inventory tracks the Cosmetic
Accessories owned and equipped by the child.

When a game is completed for the first time, the system
records its completion, awards the corresponding journey
points, and grants either coins or a cosmetic accessory.
Game Progress records whether the reward has been claimed
to prevent duplicate rewards when a completed game is
replayed.

Journey Progress summarizes individual Game Progress
records to calculate accumulated journey points and
completed levels. The coin balance is maintained
separately because coins may be spent in the
character customization shop.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}

\progname{} follows a data-minimization approach,
collecting and retaining only the information necessary
to support character customization, journey progression,
rewards, user preferences, and local game progress.
The application's privacy and data management
practices are outlined below:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Data Collection:}
    The application does not require patient accounts,
    medical records, appointment details, or personally
    identifying information. Optional nicknames are
    used solely for personalization and do not need
    to correspond to the child's legal name.

    \item \textbf{Character Selection and Customization:}
    The application stores the selected character,
    owned cosmetic accessories, and equipped accessories
    locally. Character customization does not require
    registration or the collection of personal information.

    \item \textbf{Game and Journey Progress:}
    The application stores completion status, journey
    points, and reward claim status for each preparation
    game. Total points and completed levels are calculated
    from these records and displayed on the personal
    scoreboard. This information does not represent
    clinical performance or visual ability.

    \item \textbf{Rewards and Virtual Currency:}
    In-game coins and cosmetic accessories are earned
    through game completion. Coins may be exchanged
    for accessories within the application, while
    some accessories are awarded directly.
    Coin balances and owned accessories are stored
    locally. No real-money purchases or external
    payment services are required.

    \item \textbf{Data Storage and Protection:}
    User preferences, character selections, game
    progress, coin balances, and accessory inventories
    shall be stored locally on the user's device
    and protected from unauthorized access by other
    applications. The application shall not transmit
    this information to external servers or require
    user accounts.

    \item \textbf{Data Retention and Deletion:}
    Locally stored information is retained until
    deleted by the caregiver or through application
    removal, subject to the device's backup and
    restoration behaviour. Caregivers can clear
    stored preferences, character selections,
    game progress, points, coin balances, and
    accessory inventories through the application's
    data management functionality.

    \item \textbf{Consent and Access:}
    The application does not require registration
    or the collection of personal information
    to access its preparation games. Caregivers
    acknowledge the application's educational
    purpose during initial setup. All games remain
    available regardless of whether previous
    levels have been completed.

    \item \textbf{Scoreboard Privacy:}
    The personal scoreboard displays only the child's
    accumulated journey points, completed levels,
    and current coin balance. It does not compare
    scores with other children or require an
    online leaderboard.

    \item \textbf{Offline Functionality:}
    Procedure games, characters, cosmetic accessories,
    and reward definitions are available locally.
    Game progress, rewards, accessory purchases,
    and character customization do not require
    an Internet connection or external server.

    \item \textbf{Research and Regulatory Compliance:}
    Any personal information collected separately
    for research or usability evaluation is outside
    the application's operational data storage
    and is subject to applicable institutional
    ethics and privacy requirements.

\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\label{non-func-req}
% ==============================================================

The following non-functional requirements define the quality attributes
and constraints of the application. Each requirement includes a rationale
and a measurable fit criterion.

\subsection{Look and Feel Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Layout}:] Interactive controls that serve the same function shall remain in consistent screen positions across all screens where they appear.

\textbf{Rationale:} Consistent control placement helps children locate and recognize controls without relying on text labels. Maintaining consistent positions across different activities and game features reduces confusion and supports independent navigation.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A design review confirms that controls serving the same function remain within \constref{CONTROL_POSITION_TOLERANCE_MM} millimetres of their designated positions across all applicable screens.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_ChildSafe}:]
No image, animation, narration, or sound shall be frightening, or show pain or distress beyond the sensations in the approved script.

\textbf{Rationale:} The clinic is already unfamiliar to the child, so the application must not add to the fear it is meant to reduce. Child-life specialists know what frightens children of this age, and the team does not.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A child-life specialist reviews all content against a checklist, and no finding is unresolved at release.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Usability and Humanity Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_TouchTarget}:]
Every interactive element shall be at least \constref{TOUCH_TARGET_MIN} millimetres wide and high on screen.

\textbf{Rationale:} The Apple and Android guidance minimums are about 7 mm and 9 mm \cite{AppleHIG, MaterialDesign}. Children aged 3 to 7 have less precise motor control, so a larger size is used. The value is to be confirmed in usability testing.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Measured on each reference device, every interactive element meets this size.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Exit}:]
A control that returns to the home menu shall be reachable with one touch from every screen of an activity.

\textbf{Rationale:} A child or parent who chooses the wrong activity, or wants to stop, needs a way out that does not depend on reading. Without one, the child may feel trapped, which works against the goal of reassurance.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} On every screen of every activity, a tester reaches the home menu with one touch.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Usability}:] A first-time user from the defined user characteristics shall be able to use the application's core features without reading instructions or receiving prior training.

\textbf{Rationale:} The application is intended for use at home with little or no assistance. Users should be able to understand and interact with its core features independently, supporting engagement and reducing reliance on parental guidance.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least \constref{UNAIDED_RATE_MIN}\% of participants shall successfully complete a predefined core task without verbal assistance, with a median completion time of at most \constref{ACTIVITY_PAIR_MAX_MIN} minutes. Participants aged 3--4 may have a parent present who does not explain or guide the task.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Language}:]
Every narrated sentence shall have at most \constref{SENTENCE_MAX_WORDS} words and use words familiar to a child aged 3 to 7, and text in the adult area shall be at or below Grade \constref{ADULT_TEXT_MAX_GRADE} reading level.

\textbf{Rationale:} Young children follow short sentences in familiar words, and long ones are lost. Adults vary in reading ability, and Grade 8 matches the defined user characteristics. The sentence limit is to be confirmed with the child-life specialists.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A count over all scripts finds no narrated sentence over \constref{SENTENCE_MAX_WORDS} words, a child-life specialist finds no unfamiliar word, and a readability formula gives a grade of at most \constref{ADULT_TEXT_MAX_GRADE} for each adult-area text.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Accessibility}:]
The application shall meet the WCAG 2.2 Level AA success criteria that apply to a native mobile application.

\textbf{Rationale:} Children who attend an eye clinic may have low vision, and some will have hearing or motor differences. WCAG is the accepted benchmark \cite{WCAG22}, and the same duty applies in Ontario \cite{AODA2005}.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} An accessibility audit against the applicable criteria finds no failure.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Colour}:]
Every state and prompt shall remain distinguishable without colour, and for users with common forms of colour vision deficiency.

\textbf{Rationale:} Colour vision deficiency is common enough that some children and parents will have it, and it is more likely among users of an eye service. Relying on colour alone, for example green for correct and red for wrong, would hide information from them.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Screens viewed under protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia simulation show every state and choice as distinct by shape, position, or text as well as colour.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Startup}:]
The home menu shall appear within \constref{STARTUP_MAX_S} seconds of launch.

\textbf{Rationale:} A child looking at a blank screen may lose interest before anything starts. Low-spec devices set the limit, and the value is revisited after proof-of-concept measurements.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The median of \constref{STARTUP_TRIALS_MIN} cold starts on each low-spec device is at most \constref{STARTUP_MAX_S} seconds.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Responsiveness}:]
The system shall respond visibly to a touch on an interactive element within \constref{RESPONSE_MAX_MS} milliseconds.

\textbf{Rationale:} About 0.1 seconds is the longest delay that feels instant to a user \cite{Nielsen1993}. A child who sees no reaction will tap again, which the activities then have to handle.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} At least \constref{RESPONSE_RATE_MIN}\% of measured touches on each low-spec device receive a visible response within \constref{RESPONSE_MAX_MS} milliseconds.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Smoothness}:]
Animation shall run at no fewer than \constref{FRAME_RATE_MIN} frames per second.

\textbf{Rationale:} Choppy animation of a procedure would distract from what it shows.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} On each low-spec device, the frame rate is at least \constref{FRAME_RATE_MIN} frames per second for at least \constref{FRAME_RATE_COMPLIANCE_MIN}\% of the time over a full walkthrough and practice activity.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_AVSync}:]
Narration shall start within \constref{AV_SYNC_MAX_MS} milliseconds of the animation it accompanies.

\textbf{Rationale:} Narration that does not match the picture can teach the wrong step. The value is a practical limit that keeps the two looking together, and it is checked in the proof of concept.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Measured on each reference device across all steps, no offset exceeds this value.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_AudioSafety}:]
No sound effect or music shall have a peak level above the peak level of the narration.

\textbf{Rationale:} A sudden loud sound can frighten a child who is already anxious, and can be uncomfortable for small ears. Tying every sound to the narration level, which the parent sets with the device volume, avoids a sound that is louder than the voice.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} An audio analysis of all sound assets finds no exception.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Reliability}:]
The application shall not crash or freeze during use.

\textbf{Rationale:} A crash in front of an anxious child, or in a waiting room minutes before an appointment, harms trust in the application.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} At least \constref{CRASH_FREE_RATE_MIN}\% of at least \constref{TEST_SESSIONS_MIN} test sessions end without a crash or freeze.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_DeviceHeat}:]
The application shall not cause the device temperature to exceed \constref{DEVICE_TEMP_MAX_C} degrees Celsius during use.

\textbf{Rationale:} Young children may hold a device for long periods, and a device that becomes too hot can cause discomfort or minor injury. The limit is revisited after proof-of-concept measurements on low-spec devices.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} On each low-spec device, the device temperature stays at or below this value over a continuous session of at least \constref{HEAT_TEST_SESSION_MIN} minutes of gameplay.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Operational and Environmental Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Offline}:]
After installation, every function shall work with no network connection.

\textbf{Rationale:} Hospital waiting rooms often have poor connectivity, and that is a place where the application is useful. A network dependency would also raise privacy questions.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The full functional test suite passes with the device in airplane mode.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Portability}:]
The application shall run on iOS \constref{MIN_IOS} and later and Android \constref{MIN_ANDROID} and later, on phones and tablets with screens from \constref{SCREEN_MIN_IN} to \constref{SCREEN_MAX_IN} inches.

\textbf{Rationale:} Families use the devices they already own. These versions cover the large majority of devices in use by families and keep the supported range testable by a small team. A low-spec device is one released at least \constref{LOW_SPEC_AGE_YEARS} years before the test date that still supports the minimum version. The values are revisited after the proof of concept.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The functional tests in the VnV Plan pass on every reference device.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Installability}:]
A parent shall be able to install the application from the app store and reach the home menu without creating an account, granting permissions, or downloading further content.

\textbf{Rationale:} Every extra step is a place where a parent may give up, and a required account would conflict with the decision to collect no personal data.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} On each reference device, a tester installs the application and reaches the home menu following only the store listing, with none of those steps required.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_InstallSize}:]
The installed size of the application shall be at most \constref{INSTALL_SIZE_MAX_MB} megabytes.

\textbf{Rationale:} The limit is chosen so a family can download over a mobile connection and so older devices with little storage can hold it. The proof of concept measures the real size.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The size reported by each app store for the release build is within this limit.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Maintainability and Support Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Maintainability}:]
The effort to make any likely change in the Likely Changes section shall be less than \constref{MAINT_FRACTION} of the original development time, not counting clinical review.

\textbf{Rationale:} A change such as a new procedure should cost far less than building the first one, or the application cannot keep up with what clinicians want covered.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The recorded effort for each such change made during the project is within this fraction.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Security Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_DataProtection}:]
Stored settings and progress shall be readable only by the application, using the storage the platform keeps private to it.

\textbf{Rationale:} The nickname and progress are about a child, and no other application or person using the device should read them. The platform already provides private storage.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} On an unmodified test device, no other application and no file-sharing function can read the stored data.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Integrity}:]
Every release build shall be digitally signed and distributed only through the Apple App Store and Google Play Store.

\textbf{Rationale:} What children see must be what clinicians approved. A signed build from the official stores means the content cannot have been altered on the way to the device.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The signature of each release build verifies with the team's signing key using the platform tools, and no other distribution channel is in use.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Privacy}:]
The application shall not transmit any data off the device.

\textbf{Rationale:} Not collecting data removes most of the privacy and legal exposure for a service aimed at children, who cannot consent, and the application does not need any data to work. Evaluation measures are collected separately, with consent.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A network capture during a full test session shows no outbound request from the application.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Minimization}:]
The application shall store only the nickname, age band, display settings, and progress record.

\textbf{Rationale:} Data that is not stored cannot leak or be requested. Only these items are needed for the application to function.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Inspection of the storage after a full test session finds only these items.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Immunity}:]
Each release shall contain no third-party library with a publicly known vulnerability of high or critical severity.

\textbf{Rationale:} The application has no server and accepts no input except touch, so its main exposure is through the libraries it includes. A compromised dependency could break the privacy requirements.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A dependency vulnerability scan run on each release build reports none of that severity.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_DependencyTesting}:]
Every third-party library and engine component shall be fixed to an exact version, and each change to one shall be tested on a release build before it is included in a release.

\textbf{Rationale:} The application depends on software the team does not control, and an unexpected update or incompatibility could cause crashes or wrong behaviour in the activities that prepare the child. Fixing versions means each release contains only what was tested.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The dependency list gives an exact version for every third-party library and engine component, and the functional test suite passes on the release build after each dependency change, with no finding unresolved at release.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cultural and Political Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Inclusivity}:]
Content shall not assume a child's family structure, culture, religion, gender, or ethnicity.

\textbf{Rationale:} Families using the service come from many backgrounds, and a child who does not see themselves in the content, or is shown a family unlike theirs, may feel left out when the aim is reassurance.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The review of child-facing content includes an inclusivity checklist, and no finding is unresolved at release.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Legal Requirements}

\begin{itemize}

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_NoClaims}:]
The application, its notices, and its store listings shall make no claim to diagnose, screen, treat, or monitor any condition.

\textbf{Rationale:} Software that makes such claims may be regulated as a medical device \cite{HealthCanadaSaMD}. Such a claim would also encourage a parent to treat the practice as a vision test and miss a real problem.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Two team members and a clinician review every child-facing text, adult-facing text, and store listing against a checklist, and find no such claim.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_Licensing}:]
Every third-party asset and library shall have a recorded licence that permits redistribution under the project's chosen software licence.

\textbf{Rationale:} Assets from a public store may forbid redistribution or require terms that conflict with the project's chosen licence. A removal for a licence breach would take the application away from families.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} The licence log lists every third-party asset and library with its licence, and has no gap.

\item[\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\textbf{NFR\thenfrnum}\label{NFR_StoreCompliance}:]
The application shall meet the Apple and Google policies for children's applications listed in the project's standards table.

\textbf{Rationale:} The application is aimed at children, and the policies call for parental gates and limit advertising, outside links, and third-party data collection \cite{AppleGuidelines, GooglePlayPolicy}. Non-compliance would lead to rejection or removal.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Both app stores approve the release build for a children's audience, and every item on the pre-submission policy checklist passes.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope}

The scope is focused on preparing children aged 3 to 7 for common paediatric
ophthalmology procedures. Diagnosis, treatment, and other clinical functions
were excluded because they are outside the main goal of the application and
would add requirements that are not needed for preparation. The initial set
of procedures is also limited so that the team can focus on making each
activity clear, accessible, and reliable. Features such as AR and personalized
preparation are considered stretch goals rather than part of the minimum
scope.

\subsection{Data Requirements}

The data requirements were developed by considering how
ophthalmology procedures and their preparation games should
be represented. Initially, the team considered a one to many
relationship where each procedure could have multiple
preparation activities. However, since the team had not established
whether multiple distinct games would be developed for
each procedure, a simpler one to one relationship was selected.
Each procedure is represented by one game, which may contain
multiple smaller exercises or rounds.

The team also considered two approaches for selecting preparation
games: allowing caregivers to manually choose games based
on clinical recommendations, or having caregivers enter a
clinician provided code to automatically select the appropriate
games. The team consulted the project supervisor to determine
which approach would be more practical in a clinical setting.

The supervisor recommended making all games accessible,
since the procedures required during an examination may
change unexpectedly. The supervisor also suggested presenting the
games as a journey with levels, rewards, a personal
scoreboard, and character customization.

Based on this feedback, the team removed the caregiver configuration
and recommendation code entities from the data requirements.
Instead, entities supporting character selection, 
game progress, journey progression, and rewards
were introduced. All user data is stored locally to maintain
offline functionality and minimize the collection of
personal information.

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}

This section explains decisions that affect multiple non-functional requirements. Individual
requirement rationales are provided alongside their respective requirements.

The main non-functional requirements support three priorities: child-friendly
interaction, reliable use, and privacy. Since the application is designed for
children aged 3 to 7, requirements such as \nfrref{NFR_TouchTarget},
\nfrref{NFR_Layout}, and \nfrref{NFR_Usability} ensure that activities are
easy to navigate and complete. \nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe} and
\nfrref{NFR_Language} ensure that the content is appropriate for the target
age group.

Accessibility is also important so that children with different needs can
use the application. \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility} and \nfrref{NFR_Colour}
address accessibility needs, while \nfrref{NFR_AVSync} and
\nfrref{NFR_AudioSafety} help keep audio and animations clear during
activities.

Since the application may be used at home or before an appointment,
\nfrref{NFR_Offline} allows activities to work without internet access.
\nfrref{NFR_Responsiveness}, \nfrref{NFR_Smoothness}, and
\nfrref{NFR_Reliability} help prevent delays and interruptions that could
affect the experience. \nfrref{NFR_Portability} and
\nfrref{NFR_Installability} also make the application easier to access
across supported devices.

Finally, privacy and security are especially important because the
application is intended for children. \nfrref{NFR_Minimization} limits
the information stored, \nfrref{NFR_Privacy} prevents data from being
transmitted off the device, and \nfrref{NFR_DataProtection} protects
locally stored data. \nfrref{NFR_Integrity} and \nfrref{NFR_Immunity}
help protect the application from unauthorized changes and vulnerable
dependencies. Other requirements, including \nfrref{NFR_Inclusivity},
\nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}, \nfrref{NFR_Licensing}, and
\nfrref{NFR_StoreCompliance}, address inclusive content, the application's
medical scope, and distribution requirements.
