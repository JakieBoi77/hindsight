\section{Introduction} 
% ==============================================================

This document is the \termmark{softwarerequirementsspecificationsrs}Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for \progname{}, a mobile game
that prepares children aged 3 to 7 years for \termmark{paediatricophthalmology}paediatric ophthalmology appointments.
This document defines the expected behaviour, scope, and requirements of \progname{},
establishing a basis for implementation, verification, and validation by describing what the system
is expected to accomplish and the conditions under which it will be used.

This document follows the IEEE SRS template provided in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate},
with guidance from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 \cite{ISOIECIEEE291482018} for requirements engineering and
ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 \cite{ISOIECIEEE152882023} for systems life-cycle management. The team made
modifications to the original template to better suit the project's needs. In Section~\ref{non-func-req}
(\termmark{nonfunctionalrequirementnfr}Non-Functional Requirements), the requirements are organized according to the Volere template provided
in the course repository \cite{SmithCapTemplate}. The AI and Machine Learning Requirements section was
also removed, as it is not applicable to the project. A Notations and Conventions section
(Section~\ref{sec_NotationsConventions}) was added to explain the identifiers and symbolic constants.

\subsection{Purpose}
\label{purpose}

The purpose of \progname{} is to reduce anxiety and uncertainty in children before paediatric
ophthalmology appointments by providing \termmark{accessibility}accessible, age-appropriate preparation for common eye
examination procedures. \progname{} aims to improve children's preparedness and willingness to participate while helping
parents and healthcare professionals prepare them for appointments. Intended outcomes include
reduced anxiety, improved cooperation, sustained engagement across the target age range,
more effective preparation at home, and greater clinical efficiency. The application complements,
rather than replaces, professional guidance and medical examinations.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec_Scope}

\progname{} is a mobile application intended to help children aged 3 to 7 years become familiar
with common paediatric ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment. The application
will present preparation activities as age-appropriate interactive games that allow children to
practice actions and learn what they may experience during an \termmark{eyeexamination}eye examination.

The primary users are children preparing for ophthalmology appointments, with parents or guardians
supporting their use of the application. \termmark{ophthalmologist}Ophthalmologists and \termmark{childlifespecialist}child-life specialists are secondary
\termmark{stakeholder}stakeholders who may provide clinical and developmental guidance during development and may use
the application as part of a broader preparation process.

The application will provide interactive activities corresponding to common ophthalmology
procedures, provide feedback during activities, track completion of preparation activities, and
allow users to repeat activities when needed. The application will be designed to support
preparation before an appointment, including preparation in a home environment.

The expected benefits are increased familiarity with ophthalmology procedures, reduced uncertainty
and anxiety, improved cooperation during examinations, sustained engagement, and potentially
shorter or less difficult clinical appointments.

\progname{} will not diagnose medical conditions, provide medical advice, replace an ophthalmologist,
or determine whether a child requires a particular medical procedure. The application will not
replace an actual clinical examination. Medical decisions will remain the responsibility of qualified
healthcare professionals.

The initial system will focus on preparation for common ophthalmology procedures and will not
attempt to cover every possible procedure or clinical situation. Features identified as stretch
goals, such as \termmark{augmentedrealityar}augmented reality simulations and personalized preparation based on appointment
information, are outside the minimum required scope of the initial system.

\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{sec_Stakeholders}
A stakeholder is anyone with an interest in the system and can be affected by the system. 
Stakeholders are grouped below using the categories of the IEEE template. Members of the 
development team are not listed because team members are not stakeholders of their own product.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Customer \& Project Sponsor}

A paediatric ophthalmology researcher and clinician who proposed 
the project, and the paediatric eye clinic that will pilot the product. At the clinic, the customer
and project supervisor need children who are prepared for their examinations and evidence of whether 
the product works in reducing anxiety amongst their patients.

\item \textbf{End Users}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Children aged 3 to 7 years with an upcoming eye appointment.
    \item Caregivers (Parents, Guardians, other Adults) who will install the product on their device, set 
    up the game and play the game alongside children.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Clinicians}

Paediatric ophthalmologists and ophthalmic technicians who perform the procedures. Clinicians 
benefit from more calm and cooperative patients. They will also tell families which procedures children should 
practice and review game design as the medical experts in the system.

\item \textbf{Child-life Specialists}

They are experts on how children navigate play and the process of medical care. 
They review the language, imagery and age-appropriate design to advise on the mental, emotional, and social 
needs of patients \cite{mayoChildLifeSpecialist}.

\item \textbf{Regulatory and Ethics Bodies}

The research ethics board that approves studies with children, privacy regulators, and the app store operators 
(Apple and Google), whose policies for children's apps the product must satisfy.

\item \textbf{Eye Clinics} 

The eye care clinical system benefits when examinations take less time and fewer children need 
to repeat appointments. Families with limited resources benefit by their children receiving the same preparation 
for appointments through the free app. 

\item \textbf{Other Paediatric Health-care Professionals} 

Other paediatric health-care applications can reuse the approach as inspiration for their own tool, 
such as family doctors performing check-ups.

\item \textbf{Administrators} 

Hindsight has no server, no accounts and no administration console, so there is no administrator role
because it is not applicable to this system.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem Statement}
\label{problem-statement}

Paediatric ophthalmology appointments can be stressful and unfamiliar for children.
Procedures such as vision testing, \termmark{eyedrops}eye drops, and slit-lamp examinations may cause anxiety, particularly when children do not know what to expect.
This anxiety can make it difficult to cooperate during examinations, potentially affecting the quality and completeness of clinical assessments.
For example, a study of 298 children undergoing \termmark{cycloplegiceyeexamination}cycloplegic eye examinations found that 26\% experienced significant distress before receiving eye drops,
while uncooperative children were also more anxious and required more time to complete the examination \cite{ChiaEtAl2015}.

Children and their families therefore need adequate preparation for common ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment.
Without appropriate preparation, unfamiliar clinical experiences may contribute to increased anxiety, reduced cooperation, and longer or more difficult appointments.
This project addresses the need for an accessible, child-friendly way to help children become more familiar and comfortable with the paediatric ophthalmology experience.

\subsection{Product Vision}
\label{sec_Product_Vision}

\progname{} is an interactive mobile application designed to help
children aged 3--7 feel more comfortable and prepared for paediatric
ophthalmology appointments. Through age appropriate games and activities,
the application introduces children to common procedures, including
vision testing, eye drop administration, and slit lamp examinations.
By allowing children and their families to become familiar with these
procedures before their appointments, \progname{} aims to reduce
anxiety, improve cooperation during examinations, and support a
more efficient clinical experience.

The primary goals of \progname{} are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduce Children's Anxiety:}
    Help children understand what to expect during common ophthalmology
    procedures, reducing anxiety associated with unfamiliar clinical
    experiences.

    \item \textbf{Improve Children's Cooperation During Examinations:}
    Prepare children to participate more cooperatively in clinical
    examinations, reducing interruptions and repeated attempts
    during procedures.

    \item \textbf{Maintain Engagement Across Age Groups:}
    Provide engaging and age appropriate activities that encourage
    children across the target age range to actively participate
    in their preparation.

    \item \textbf{Enable Effective At-Home Preparation:}
    Allow children and their families to independently prepare
    for upcoming ophthalmology appointments through accessible
    and understandable activities.

    \item \textbf{Improve Clinical Efficiency:}
    Support more efficient ophthalmology examinations by improving
    children's preparedness and reducing difficulties associated
    with completing clinical procedures.
\end{itemize}

These goals establish the intended outcomes of \progname{} and provide
the foundation for the functional and non-functional requirements
defined in this document.

The touch input and audio-visual output of the child and caregiver physically

  \item Enters local preferences, such as the child's optional nickname and age

  \item Confirms actions that cannot be undone, such as resetting saved data
    (\rref{FR_ResetJourney}).
  \item Reads the procedure guide and the notice.
  \item Supervises younger children as needed.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Device operating system and hardware:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Delivers touch events and life-cycle events, such as the application
    being sent to the background, closed or resumed.
  \item Displays images and plays audio at the volume the caregiver sets.
  \item Stores and returns the application's saved data in storage that only
    the application can read (\nfrref{NFR_DataProtection}).
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}
  \item Approve the procedure content before each release. This includes the

  \item Confirm that the content is suitable for children aged 3 to 7
    (\nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe}).
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}
  \item Delivers the signed installation package and later updates
    (\nfrref{NFR_Integrity}, \conref{C_StoreCompliance}). The package contains

  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{\progname{}:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Presents the approved procedure games and caregiver information without
    a network connection (\conref{C_OfflineOperation}).
  \item Accepts only the touch inputs expected on the current screen.
  \item Ignores repeated or accidental touches that would skip procedure
    information.
  \item Records progress, rewards, character choices and preferences
    (Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec}).
  \item Stores no identifying information or clinical records
    (\conref{C_PatientPrivacy}).
  \item Checks saved data when it is loaded. If the data is missing or invalid,
    the application recovers without blocking access to any level
    (\rref{FR_CorruptData}).
  \item Stops all audio and animation of a level when the child exits it.
  \item Keeps the child's progress when the application is interrupted
    (\rref{FR_Suspend}, \rref{FR_PersistState}).
  \item Never transmits data off the device (\nfrref{NFR_Privacy}).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Context of use:} \progname{} is used for health education. Families
use it at home before an appointment or in a clinic waiting room. It is not
mission-critical. It is also not safety-critical in the sense of controlling
equipment or informing clinical decisions. It makes no diagnostic or treatment
claim (\nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}).

However, its users are young children in a medical setting. Some failures can
increase a child's anxiety or reduce cooperation at the appointment, which is
the opposite of the application's purpose. Examples are inaccurate or
frightening content, hidden sensations, punishing feedback, \termmark{privacy}privacy failures,
and a caregiver treating the game as medical advice. The
\hadoc{Hazard Analysis} document analyses these hazards. The resulting safety and security requirements
are included in this SRS. Requirements that address these hazards receive a
fuller rationale than other requirements.

\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

The users are different from the intended readers of this document Section~\ref{sec_IntendedReader}. 
The characteristics below are used to set the non-functional usability requirements in 
Section~\ref{sec_UsabilityReqs}. The characteristics are also illustrated by the personas in 
Section~\ref{sec_Personas}.

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Children}}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Aged 3 to 7 years. Many cannot read and older children 
    read simple words. All instructions must therefore be spoken or narrated and shown with pictures
    or visual guides.
    \item Actively developing fine motor control. Children in this age range tap imprecisely, often 
    with several fingers, and may tap repeatedly or not at all.
    \item Have short attention spans, which grow with age.
    \item May have reduced vision at the time of use because they are eye patients. Therefore, some 
    wear patches or glasses. Additionally, in the waiting room their pupils may be dilated, 
    making the screen look blurry and bright. Some have colour vision deficiencies.
    \item May already be anxious about the appointment and respond strongly to praise and 
    to perceived failure.
    \item May be highly dependent on caregiver, making the child shy to other adults and the 
    examinations necessary to take place during the appointment or try things on their own, like 
    the EyeCanDo application in the waiting room.
    \item The child may not have used technology before, making the digital game seem like a steep 
    learning curve adding in a different element of anxiety or nervousness.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Caregivers}}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Adults with varied education, technical experience and English proficiency. 
    \item Adults with varied accesibility needs, like their own vision impairment (notable in an eye clinic).
    \item They can operate a smartphone app store and read simple English instructions.
    \item Often stressed or short of time and may use Hindsight in a busy waiting room alongside 
    their child.
    \item Have no clinical training.
    \item Be tired after a full day of work, other activities, or general external factors (e.g. family 
    illness, other child responsibilities) affecting level of patience.
    \item They can legally consent to examinations, share medical history, or approve data collection 
    on behalf of their children.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Clinicians}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eye-care professionals with intensive clinical procedure knowledge.
    \item Eye-care professionals who use and administer specialized equipment such as the slit-lamp, 
    Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) scanners for eye photos, eye drop medications, and other 
    vision tests equipment.
    \item Have relatively little time per patient as appointment slots are often full, so an 
    introductory interaction of \progname{} with the patient must be within 1 minute.
    \item Have large schedules packed with patient appointments or other healthcare responsibilities, 
    so training in \progname{} must be within 5 minutes.
    \item Experience frequent interruptions from restless children and have to face 
    multiple caregiver questions.
    \item Must abide by standards and ethics set by regulatory bodies regarding patient privacy, 
    security, and personal information.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Child-life Specialists}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Certified and trained in child development, play, and medical care navigation.
    \item Have clinical experience and clinical training.
    \item Can share visibility of the child's coping plan with clinicians to everyone use 
    consistent language and approaches for the child
    \item They can operate a smartphone app store and read English instructions with 
    professional fluency.
    \item Provide information, support, and guidance to parents and family members about 
    examinations and medical procedures \cite{mayoChildLifeSpecialist}.
    \item Collaborative skills with the clinicians to coordinate and manage care \cite{mayoChildLifeSpecialist}.
    \item Must abide by standards and ethics set by regulatory bodies regarding patient privacy, 
    security, and personal information.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Constraints}
\label{sec_Systen_Constraints}

\begin{reqlist}{C}
\item[\cdef{C_MobilePlatforms}]
\progname{} shall operate on consumer smartphones and tablets running
\termmark{ios}iOS or \termmark{android}Android.

\textit{Rationale:} Patient families and clinics may use mobile devices running
either operating system. Supporting both enables preparation
at home and in clinic without assuming a particular platform.

\item[\cdef{C_ApplicationLicensing}]
Third-party assets and libraries included in \progname{} shall have
licenses allowing their use and redistribution within the application.
Required copyright notices, licenses, and attributions shall be retained.

\textit{Rationale:} The application's source code is licensed under the
MIT license, while third-party materials have their own licensing.
These conditions must be satisfied when the application is released.

\item[\cdef{C_RepositoryLicensing}]
Third-party materials present in the \progname{} GitHub
repository shall have licenses allowing redistribution in the form committed to the repository.

\textit{Rationale:} Permission to distribute an asset in
an application does not necessarily mean redistribution of its raw
files in the project repository is permitted. Repository contents must satisfy
the applicable licensing conditions (separately from the compiled application).

\item[\cdef{C_CrossPlatformEngine}]
\progname{} shall be built using a game engine with native support
for cross-platform deployment to iOS and Android.

\textit{Rationale:} Native deployment support satisfies \conref{C_MobilePlatforms}. The team has
currently selected the Unity game engine, but the selected engine may
change provided that it satisfies this constraint.

\item[\cdef{C_OfflineOperation}]
\progname{} shall provide all included preparation modules and their
required assets without internet connection after installation.

\textit{Rationale:} Families must be able to prepare at home and
in clinic, where connectivity may be poor or unavailable.

\item[\cdef{C_NoNetworkCommunications}]
\progname{} shall operate as a standalone local application and shall
not initiate network communications during operation, including
through included third-party libraries.

\textit{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require external
services or data transit. Prohibiting network
communications also prevents included libraries from transmitting usage
data. Downloading and updating the application through its distribution
channels are outside normal application operation (i.e., a patient progressing through the application's
preparation modules).

\item[\cdef{C_PatientPrivacy}]
\progname{} shall not request, collect, or store identifying
information or clinical records, and shall not integrate with clinic
information systems.

\textit{Rationale:} Preparation activities do not require patient
records. Limiting application data to local gameplay, like character selection and scores,
supports preparation without requiring identifying information.

\item[\cdef{C_StoreCompliance}]
The iOS and Android releases of \progname{} shall comply with the \termmark{appleappstore}Apple App Store and Google Play submission
requirements, respectively, including applicable policies for applications meant for children, at time of submission.

\textit{Rationale:} These marketplaces are the planned distribution
channels through which families and clinics will install the application. Compliance
with their submission requirements is necessary for distribution to the target users.
\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Persona 1: Four-Year-Old Paediatric Patient}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Name:} Noah.

\item \textbf{Description:} Noah is a four-year-old boy who has been referred
to an ophthalmologist after his mother noticed that he appeared to have
difficulty seeing objects on screens and paper. He often sits close to the
TV or brings the family's tablet close to his face while playing.
He cannot read independently, but enjoys sharing children's books with his
mother and playing simple tablet games that involve tapping and
dragging objects. During previous visits to the family doctor, he has cried
and resisted participating in unfamiliar activities. Holding his favourite
stuffed animal or playing a familiar game helps him settle.
He feels proud when he receives a sticker at the end of the appointment,
recognizing his participation.

\item \textbf{Goals:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Understand what will happen during the eye appointment, including what
    procedures might feel like, and feel less worried about unfamiliar equipment/procedures.
    \item Know what he can do during each preparation activity and examination

    \item Feel proud of participating, and show his mother the activities he completed.

\item \textbf{Frustrations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Instructions presented only as text, which he cannot read independently.
    \item Loud environments that make him feel overwhelmed or make spoken
    instructions hard to hear.
    \item Small or similar symbols that he struggles to distinguish between
    in games.
    \item Unfamiliar controls that give him no clear guidance
    when he makes a mistake.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Technical Ability:} Noah is familiar with basic tapping and
swiping on the family tablet. He recognizes some familiar symbols, but cannot read instructions independently.
He follows short spoken instructions and visual demonstrations, with help
from his mother when something is too unfamiliar. He needs guidance for
complex gestures or navigation with many steps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Use Cases}
\label{sec_UseCases}

The following \termmark{usecaseuc}use cases describe how children and caregivers use
\progname{} to explore ophthalmology preparation games through a
journey of levels. All procedure game levels remain accessible regardless
of whether earlier levels have been completed. Children may choose a
character, earn \termmark{journeypoints}journey points, receive \termmark{ingamecoins}in game coins or cosmetic items,
and use owned items to customize their character. The \termmark{personalscoreboard}personal scoreboard
shows the child's own progress, not a ranking against other children.
The reward amounts, character designs, available accessories, and specific
gameplay interactions described below are initial design proposals that
will be refined with the project team and clinical supervisors.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_FirstUse}
\subsection{\ucheading{UC_FirstUse}: Complete Initial Application Setup}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Caregiver

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The application is installed on a supported device.
    \item The application is being opened for the first time, and no
    caregiver acknowledgment has been recorded.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The caregiver opens \progname{}.
    \item The system displays a welcome screen explaining that the
    application helps children become familiar with eye examinations
    through interactive games.
    \item The system displays a notice stating that the application is
    an educational preparation aid and not a substitute for professional
    medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
    \item The caregiver reads the notice and selects the acknowledgment
    control.
    \item The system records the acknowledgment locally.
    \item The system displays an introduction to the journey, explaining
    that all procedure game levels are available and that children can
    earn points and cosmetic rewards as they play.
    \item The caregiver continues to the home screen, where the child
    can choose a character and start exploring the journey.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Notice Not Acknowledged:} If the caregiver does not
    acknowledge the notice, the system does not complete initial setup.
    \item \textbf{Setup Interrupted:} If the application closes before
    acknowledgment is saved, the system displays the notice again on
    the next launch.
    \item \textbf{Returning User:} If acknowledgment was previously
    recorded, the system opens the home screen without repeating setup.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The caregiver has been informed of the application's purpose
    and limitations.
    \item Acknowledgment is recorded locally, and the child can access
    the journey without creating an account.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{\termmark{functionalrequirementfr}Functional Requirements}

\rref{FR_MedicalNotice}, \rref{FR_RecordAck}.

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_ChooseCharacter}
\subsection{\ucheading{UC_ChooseCharacter}: Choose a Journey Character}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Child

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Initial application setup has been completed.
    \item The application is displaying the home screen or character
    selection screen.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The child selects the character button on the home screen.

    after skipping some exercises, the system still awards the
    predefined participation based reward.

    previously acquired accessories without network access.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Other Level:} The child selects Eye-Drop or
    Slit-Lamp Preparation; its built in content also works offline.
    \item \textbf{Offline Purchase:} The child purchases a cosmetic
    item using sufficient locally recorded coins; the balance and
    inventory update without contacting an external server.
    \item \textbf{Exit Early:} The child exits before the level ending;
    the level remains In Progress and no first completion reward
    is issued.
    \item \textbf{Connection Lost Midgame:} If a connection is
    disabled while the application is already running, gameplay,
    scoring, and locally stored rewards continue functioning.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Journey progress and any earned rewards are saved locally.
    \item Levels, the personal scoreboard, and character customization
    function without an Internet connection or external account.
\end{itemize}

\section{Functional Requirements}
\label{sec_FR}

The functional requirements state what \progname{} shall do. They refine the
use cases of Section~\ref{sec_UseCases}.

\progname{} presents a single journey. The journey has one level for each
procedure, such as vision testing, eye drops, or the slit-lamp examination.
Any level can be played at any time. Finishing a level for the first time gives
a level reward.

This section has two parts. Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec} first defines a
formal model of the player's progress and rewards.
Section~\ref{sec_FRList} then states the functional requirements. A
requirement may use a name from the model, and it then cites the definition or
theorem it relies on. Where a requirement and the model describe the same
behaviour, the model is authoritative.

The format of each level is not yet decided. The model and the requirements
therefore describe the journey and the progress through its levels, and not
what happens inside a level.

Several requirements come from the safety and security requirements of the
\hadoc{Hazard Analysis}. Those requirements are stated here as functional requirements
when they describe what the system does, and in
Section~\ref{non-func-req} when they describe a quality of the system or the
release process. Each requirement that comes from the Hazard Analysis has a
\textit{Hazard Analysis} line. The line names the safety and security
requirement (SR) and the hazard (H) it addresses, using the numbering of the
Hazard Analysis. Each SR and H number links to that item in the published
Hazard Analysis. Table~\ref{tab:HA2FRtm} traces each safety and security
requirement to the functional requirements.

\subsection{Formal Model}
\label{sec_FormalSpec}

This section formalizes the most critical
part of \progname{}. This is the child's progress through the levels and the
rewards for finishing them. It is used every time a child plays, and an error
here harms an anxious child. Examples are a locked level, a reward that is lost
or given twice, or progress that disappears. The shop, the characters and the
accessories are not modelled. Their requirements are simple enough to state in
words (Section~\ref{sec_FRCharacters}).

\termmark{definitiond}Definitions (D1, D2, \ldots) fix the model. \termmark{theoremt}Theorems (T1, T2, \ldots) state the
properties that the requirements depend on. Each theorem has a short proof and
lists the requirements it specifies.

\textbf{Notation.} Names in italics, such as $\mathit{coins}$, are names in the
model. Values in a sans-serif font, such as \val{Completed}, are values.
$\mathbb{N}$ is the natural numbers, including $0$. A primed name, such as
$\mathit{coins}'$, is the value after an operation.

\textbf{Game content.} The team defines the game content before release, and it
does not change while the application runs. $\mathit{Levels}$ is the finite,
non-empty set of levels, one for each procedure (Procedure Game in the data
dictionary). For each level, $\mathit{points}(\mathit{level}) \in \mathbb{N}$
and $\mathit{coinReward}(\mathit{level}) \in \mathbb{N}$ are the journey points
and coins for finishing it the first time (Reward Definition). A level reward
may also include an accessory. It is given at the same moment as the coins, so
every result below about coins also holds for it.

\begin{definition}{Level status}{def_Status}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{Status} = \{\val{NotStarted}, \val{InProgress}, \val{Completed}\}
\label{eq_Status}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\val{NotStarted} means the level has never been started. \val{InProgress}
means it has been started but not finished. \val{Completed} means it has been
finished at least once.

\begin{definition}{Player state}{def_Player}
The player state is the pair
$(\mathit{status}, \mathit{coins})$, where
$\mathit{status} : \mathit{Levels} \to \mathit{Status}$ and
$\mathit{coins} \in \mathbb{N}$.

In the \emph{new player state}, $\mathit{status}(\mathit{level}) =
\val{NotStarted}$ for every level, and $\mathit{coins} = 0$.
\end{definition}

These are completion\_status and coin\_balance in the data dictionary. A
level's reward is given exactly when it becomes \val{Completed}, so
reward\_claimed is \val{true} exactly when the status is \val{Completed}.

\begin{definition}{Scoreboard values}{def_Scoreboard}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{totalPoints} = \sum_{\substack{\mathit{level} \in \mathit{Levels}\\
  \mathit{status}(\mathit{level}) = \val{Completed}}}
  \mathit{points}(\mathit{level})
\label{eq_TotalPoints}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{completedLevels} = |\{\mathit{level} \in \mathit{Levels} \mid
  \mathit{status}(\mathit{level}) = \val{Completed}\}|
\label{eq_Completed}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

These values are calculated from the player state and are not stored.

\begin{definition}{Operations}{def_Ops}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.17\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.42\textwidth}@{}}
\textbf{Operation} & \textbf{Guard} & \textbf{Effect}\\
\hline
$\mathit{start}(\mathit{level})$ & always &
  if $\mathit{status}(\mathit{level}) \neq \val{Completed}$, then
  $\mathit{status}'(\mathit{level}) = \val{InProgress}$\\
$\mathit{finish}(\mathit{level})$ &
  $\mathit{status}(\mathit{level}) = \val{InProgress}$ &
  $\mathit{status}'(\mathit{level}) = \val{Completed}$ and
  $\mathit{coins}' = \mathit{coins} + \mathit{coinReward}(\mathit{level})$\\
$\mathit{exit}(\mathit{level})$ & always & none\\
$\mathit{reset}$ & always & the new player state\\
\end{tabular}
\end{definition}

$\mathit{start}$ is used when the child starts a level, $\mathit{finish}$ when
the child reaches the end of a level, $\mathit{exit}$ when the child leaves a
level early, and $\mathit{reset}$ when the caregiver resets the journey. If
the guard is false, nothing changes. A part of the player state that the
effect does not name keeps its value.

\begin{theorem}{Every level can always be started}{thm_Start}
For every level and every player state, $\mathit{start}(\mathit{level})$ takes
effect. Afterwards the level is \val{Completed} if it was \val{Completed}
before, and \val{InProgress} otherwise.
\end{theorem}
\textit{Proof.} The guard of $\mathit{start}$ is always true, and its effect is
as stated (\defref{def_Ops}). \hfill $\square$

\textit{Specifies:} \rref{FR_StartLevel}, \rref{FR_Replay}.

\begin{theorem}{Level status state machine}{thm_StatusMachine}
The status of a level changes only along the arrows below. In particular, a
\val{Completed} level stops being \val{Completed} only by $\mathit{reset}$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  font=\small,
  state/.style={draw, rounded corners=6pt, minimum width=2.6cm,
    minimum height=0.9cm, align=center, fill=blue!6},
  tr/.style={-{Stealth[length=2.5mm]}, thick},
  lbl/.style={font=\scriptsize, align=center}
]
\fill (-2.4, 0) circle (3pt);
\node[state] (ns) at (0, 0) {\val{NotStarted}};
\node[state] (ip) at (4.4, 0) {\val{InProgress}};
\node[state] (c) at (8.8, 0) {\val{Completed}};
\draw[tr] (-2.3, 0) -- (ns);
\draw[tr] (ns) -- node[lbl, above] {$\mathit{start}$} (ip);
\draw[tr] (ip) -- node[lbl, above] {$\mathit{finish}$} (c);
\draw[tr] (ip) to[bend left=25] node[lbl, above] {$\mathit{reset}$} (ns);
\draw[tr] (c) to[bend left=30] node[lbl, below] {$\mathit{reset}$} (ns);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\end{theorem}
\textit{Proof.} Read the effects in \defref{def_Ops}. $\mathit{start}$
changes only a status that is not \val{Completed}, to \val{InProgress}.
$\mathit{finish}$ changes only \val{InProgress}, to \val{Completed}.
$\mathit{reset}$ makes every level \val{NotStarted}. $\mathit{exit}$ changes
nothing. \hfill $\square$

\textit{Specifies:} \rref{FR_StartLevel}, \rref{FR_Replay},
\rref{FR_LevelEnd}, \rref{FR_ExitKeepsData}, \rref{FR_ResetJourney}.

\begin{theorem}{Each reward is given once}{thm_Once}
Between two resets, the first $\mathit{finish}(\mathit{level})$ after the
level is started adds $\mathit{points}(\mathit{level})$ to
$\mathit{totalPoints}$ and $\mathit{coinReward}(\mathit{level})$ to
$\mathit{coins}$. Every later $\mathit{finish}(\mathit{level})$ changes
nothing.
\end{theorem}
\textit{Proof.} The first $\mathit{finish}$ finds the level \val{InProgress},
so it makes the level \val{Completed} and adds the coins (\defref{def_Ops}).
By equation~\eqref{eq_TotalPoints}, $\mathit{totalPoints}$ now counts the
level. By \thmref{thm_StatusMachine}, the level stays \val{Completed} until a
reset, so the guard of every later $\mathit{finish}$ is false.
\hfill $\square$

\textit{Specifies:} \rref{FR_LevelEnd}, \rref{FR_NoPenalty},
\rref{FR_FirstReward}, \rref{FR_NoDuplicateReward}.

\begin{theorem}{Progress is lost only by a reset}{thm_NoLoss}
For $\mathit{start}$, $\mathit{finish}$ and $\mathit{exit}$, a \val{Completed}
level stays \val{Completed}, $\mathit{totalPoints}' \geq \mathit{totalPoints}$,
and $\mathit{coins}' \geq \mathit{coins}$. In particular, $\mathit{exit}$
changes nothing.
\end{theorem}
\textit{Proof.} The first part is \thmref{thm_StatusMachine}. Then
$\mathit{totalPoints}$ cannot decrease, by equation~\eqref{eq_TotalPoints}.
Only $\mathit{finish}$ changes $\mathit{coins}$, and it only adds to them.
$\mathit{exit}$ has no effect (\defref{def_Ops}). \hfill $\square$

Spending coins in the shop is not one of these operations, so this theorem
does not cover it.

\textit{Specifies:} \rref{FR_Replay}, \rref{FR_NoPenalty},
\rref{FR_ExitLevel}, \rref{FR_ExitKeepsData}, \rref{FR_RestartLevel}.

\subsection{Requirements}
\label{sec_FRList}

This subsection states the functional requirements, grouped by feature. Each
requirement has a rationale and a \textit{Traces} line. The \textit{Traces}
line lists the use cases the requirement refines and the definitions and
theorems of Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec} it relies on. A requirement that
helps satisfy non-functional requirements also has a \textit{Related NFRs} line

comes from the Hazard Analysis also has a \textit{Hazard Analysis} line.

\subsubsection{Setup}
\label{sec_FRSetup}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_MedicalNotice}]
Before first use, the system shall display a medical notice to the
caregiver. The notice shall state that \progname{} is an educational
preparation aid and does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis,
or treatment. The system shall not open the home screen until the caregiver
acknowledges the notice. The notice shall also be available from the caregiver
procedure guide (\rref{FR_ProcedureGuide}).

\textit{Rationale:} A caregiver could mistake the procedure content for medical guidance.
Requiring an acknowledgement makes sure the caregiver has seen what the
application is for before the child uses it.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_FirstUse}, \ucref{UC_ProcedureInformation}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MedicalDisclaimer}{10}{MisinterpretedMedicalPurpose}{2-8}.

\item[\frdef{FR_RecordAck}]
When the caregiver acknowledges the medical notice
(\rref{FR_MedicalNotice}), the system shall store the acknowledgement
locally. On later launches
with a stored acknowledgement, the system shall open the home screen without
displaying the notice.

\textit{Rationale:} The caregiver only needs to read the notice once. Showing
it on every launch would slow down every session and teach caregivers to
dismiss it without reading.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_FirstUse}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MedicalDisclaimer}{10}{MisinterpretedMedicalPurpose}{2-8}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Journey Map and Levels}
\label{sec_FRJourney}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_JourneyMap}]
The system shall display a journey map that shows every level, one for each
procedure, in order of level number. Each level
shall show its name, an illustration, and its status (\defref{def_Status}:
\val{NotStarted},
\val{InProgress}, or \val{Completed}).

\textit{Rationale:} The journey map is how the child chooses what to play. The
status of each level shows the child and caregiver what has been done so far.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_SelectGame}, \defref{def_Status}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Usability}, \nfrref{NFR_Colour}.

\item[\frdef{FR_StartLevel}]
The system shall allow the child to select any level at any time. The
status of the other levels, the journey points, and the rewards shall not
restrict this choice (\thmref{thm_Start}). When the child selects a level, the
system shall start it from the beginning and apply $\mathit{start}$
(\defref{def_Ops}). If the level was not \val{Completed}, its status shall
become \val{InProgress}.

\textit{Rationale:} The procedures at an appointment can change at short
notice. A locked level could stop a child from preparing for the procedure
they actually need. Starting from the beginning gives the child the full
context of the procedure each time. Recording \val{InProgress} shows on the
journey map that the level has been tried.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_SelectGame}, \defref{def_Ops},
\thmref{thm_Start}, \thmref{thm_StatusMachine}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AllLevelsAccessible}{11}{RestrictedLevelAccess}{2-11}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Replay}]
The system shall allow the child to replay a \val{Completed} level. The level
shall remain \val{Completed} during and after the replay.

\textit{Rationale:} Repeating a level helps a child become familiar with a
procedure. A replay must not undo the progress the child has already made.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_SelectGame}, \ucref{UC_CompleteGame},
\thmref{thm_Start}, \thmref{thm_StatusMachine},
\thmref{thm_NoLoss}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Levels}
\label{sec_FRGameplay}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_LevelContent}]
Each level shall prepare the child for one ophthalmology procedure. It shall
present at least one part of that procedure, such as a step of the procedure,
a piece of equipment, or an instruction the child may be given.

\textit{Rationale:} The purpose of the journey is preparation. A level that
does not relate to a procedure adds play time without preparing the child. The
format of each level is not yet decided, so this requirement states only what
a level must convey.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\item[\frdef{FR_DescribeSensations}]
Before a level shows a part of a procedure that the child may feel, such
as cold, stinging, a bright light, pressure, or blurry vision, the system shall
describe that sensation to the child. The description shall not be skippable.
The system shall not state or imply that a procedure will not hurt or will not
be uncomfortable.

\textit{Rationale:} A child who has been told about a sensation is not surprised by it at the
appointment. A promise that nothing will be felt would be broken at the
clinic, and the child would learn to distrust the people who prepared them.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{HonestSensations}{8}{SensationsHidden}{2-6}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ClinicsDiffer}]
Each level shall tell the child, in narration and pictures, that their
doctor may do things a little differently from the game.

\textit{Rationale:} Procedures vary between patients, clinicians, and clinics. A child who
expects small differences is less likely to be upset by them at the
appointment.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ClinicsDiffer}{6}{InaccurateProcedure}{2-5}.

\item[\frdef{FR_SpeakUp}]
Before the end of each level, the system shall tell the child that they can
tell the doctor if they feel worried or uncomfortable.

\textit{Rationale:} Knowing that they can speak up gives the child some
control during the examination, which can reduce anxiety.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame}.

\item[\frdef{FR_LevelEnd}]
When the child reaches the end of a level, the system shall display an end of
level screen and apply $\mathit{finish}$ (\defref{def_Ops}). The level's
status shall become \val{Completed}.

\textit{Rationale:} A clear ending gives the child a sense of achievement.
Recording the level as \val{Completed} lets the journey map and scoreboard
show it.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}, \defref{def_Ops}, \thmref{thm_Once},
\thmref{thm_StatusMachine}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Instructions and Feedback}
\label{sec_FRInstructions}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_SpokenInstructions}]
The system shall give every instruction to the child as narration together with
a visual demonstration. No level shall require the child to read.

\textit{Rationale:} Most children aged 3 to 7 cannot read independently.
Narration with a demonstration lets them follow without an adult reading to
them.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Usability}, \nfrref{NFR_Language}, \nfrref{NFR_AVSync}, \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}.

\item[\frdef{FR_HintOnMiss}]
When a level asks the child to do something and the child does something else,
the system shall give an encouraging hint and let the child try again.

\textit{Rationale:} A hint lets the child continue without help from an adult.
Letting the child try again keeps the focus on learning rather than on getting
it right the first time.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Usability}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{NonPunitiveFeedback}{12}{PunitiveFeedback}{2-12}.

\item[\frdef{FR_VisualCues}]
The visual cues that accompany every instruction
(\rref{FR_SpokenInstructions}) shall be shown in every state of a level. The
child shall not be able to turn them off. Any setting that changes them shall
be available only in the caregiver settings (\rref{FR_ParentGate}).

\textit{Rationale:} Visual cues are the only guidance for a child who cannot hear the
narration. A child could turn them off by accident and then be unable to
continue.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}, \ucref{UC_AdjustPreferences}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{VisualCuesSetting}{19}{VisualCuesDisabledSetting}{2-15}.

\item[\frdef{FR_NarrationFallback}]
When narration cannot be heard because the device is muted or its volume
is off, the system shall continue to give every instruction through pictures,
icons, and demonstrations. The system shall also show a prompt for the
caregiver that explains how to turn the sound on. The prompt shall not stop
the child from playing.

\textit{Rationale:} Turning the sound down must not cut the child off from the preparation
content. Deaf children and children in a quiet waiting room also need to be
able to play without sound.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}, \nfrref{NFR_AudioSafety}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{SafeAudioVisuals}{14}{UnsafeAudioVisuals}{2-16}, \hazsr{NarrationMuted}{18}{NarrationDisabledSetting}{2-14}.

\item[\frdef{FR_RevealAnswer}]
When the child gives an incorrect answer to a question, the system shall
show every correct answer within \constref{ANSWER_REVEAL_MAX_S} second. The
correct answers shall be marked with narration and with at least two cues that
do not rely on colour, such as an outline, an icon, or motion. The child shall
then be able to select a correct answer and receive encouraging feedback
before the level continues.

\textit{Rationale:} A child who leaves a question unsure of the answer may stay worried about
it. Cues that do not rely on colour or reading work for colour-blind children
and pre-readers.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Colour}, \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{CorrectAnswerReveal}{16}{CorrectAnswerReveal}{2-10}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ValidQuestions}]
When the application is built, the build process shall check that every
question has at least one correct answer. A question without a valid correct
answer shall be left out of the build and recorded in the build log, so that
it is never shown to the child.

\textit{Rationale:} A question that cannot be answered correctly would leave the child stuck
and confused. Checking the questions when the application is built removes
the problem before release.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AtLeastOneCorrectAnswer}{17}{CorrectAnswerReveal}{2-10}.

\item[\frdef{FR_NoPenalty}]
The system shall allow the child to skip any exercise in a level.
Skipping an exercise or giving an incorrect response shall not produce
negative feedback and shall not remove any points, coins, or accessories. It
shall not prevent the child from reaching the end of the level.

\textit{Rationale:} Penalties would make the game a new source of stress. Letting the child
skip an exercise lets them explore a procedure at their own pace. The reward
for finishing a level does not depend on the answers
(\thmref{thm_Once}).

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}, \thmref{thm_Once},
\thmref{thm_NoLoss}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{NonPunitiveFeedback}{12}{PunitiveFeedback}{2-12}.

\item[\frdef{FR_IgnoreRepeatedTouches}]
During a screen transition, the system shall act on at most one
navigation input. Further touches before the next screen is shown shall be
ignored. Repeated or accidental touches shall not skip the narration of
procedure information.

\textit{Rationale:} Young children often tap the screen many times. Without this rule, extra
taps could skip the explanation of a procedure that the child needs to
hear.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_VisionGame}, \ucref{UC_EyeDropGame},
\ucref{UC_SlitLampGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Responsiveness}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{PreventAccidentalInput}{3}{AccidentalInputSkipping}{2-1}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Exiting and Interruptions}
\label{sec_FRExit}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_ExitLevel}]
When the child selects the home control during a level, the system shall end
the level, apply $\mathit{exit}$ (\defref{def_Ops}), and return to the
journey map.

\textit{Rationale:} A child who is upset or bored must be able to leave at any
time. Returning to the journey map lets them choose another level.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}, \defref{def_Ops}, \thmref{thm_NoLoss}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Exit}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}.

\item[\frdef{FR_StopOnExit}]
When a level ends early, the system shall immediately stop all audio,
animation, and visual effects of that level. None of them shall continue on the
journey map.

\textit{Rationale:} Sounds or animations from a level the child has left could confuse or
upset them. Stopping everything makes leaving a level feel complete.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}, \defref{def_Ops}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Exit}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{StopAudioVisualsOnExit}{13}{AudioVisualsContinueOnExit}{2-13}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ExitKeepsData}]
Leaving a level before its end shall not change the player state
(\defref{def_Player}). In particular, it shall not give a reward or change the
status of any level.

\textit{Rationale:} Leaving early is not a failure. If leaving cost the child
progress, a child who is upset might feel pressure to keep playing.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}, \defref{def_Player},
\thmref{thm_StatusMachine}, \thmref{thm_NoLoss}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}.

\item[\frdef{FR_RestartAfterExit}]
When the child starts a level that was left before its end, or that was
interrupted because the application closed, the level shall start again from
the beginning.

\textit{Rationale:} Restarting from the beginning is simpler for a young child
than resuming in the middle of a level they may not remember.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}.

\item[\frdef{FR_RestartLevel}]
From every screen of a level, the system shall offer a way to restart the
level and a way to return to the journey map. Input that the level does not
expect shall be ignored and shall not stop the level from continuing.
Restarting a level shall keep all saved progress and rewards.

\textit{Rationale:} An unexpected input or an unfinished action must never leave the child in
a state they cannot get out of. A restart that kept nothing would make the
child lose what they had already earned.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}, \thmref{thm_NoLoss}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Exit}, \nfrref{NFR_Reliability}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Suspend}]
When the device sends the application to the background, or when an
interruption such as a phone call or a notification takes over the audio or
the screen, the system shall pause all audio and animation. When the
interruption ends, the system shall continue from where it paused.

\textit{Rationale:} A phone call or a locked screen should not make the child
miss part of a level, and sound should not keep playing in the background.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Reliability}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{NarrationMuted}{18}{NarrationDisabledSetting}{2-14}.

\item[\frdef{FR_PersistState}]
The system shall save the player state (\defref{def_Player}), the owned and
equipped accessories, and the selected character on the device each time
one of them changes, before
processing the next input. A save that fails or is interrupted shall leave the
previously saved player state unchanged. When the application is launched
again, the system shall restore the saved player state.

\textit{Rationale:} The child's progress and rewards must survive the
application closing. Saving after every change means at most the current input
can be lost. Keeping the previous save when a save fails means a failure never
replaces valid progress with damaged data.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ExitGame}, \ucref{UC_OfflineUse},
\defref{def_Player}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Reliability}, \nfrref{NFR_Offline}, \nfrref{NFR_DataProtection}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ReliableSaveData}{20}{SaveDataCorruption}{2-18}.

\item[\frdef{FR_CorruptData}]
When the saved data is missing, cannot be read, or is not valid, the system
shall replace it with the data of a new installation and continue without
failing. That data is the new player state (\defref{def_Player}), no
accessories, and no selected character.

\textit{Rationale:} A crash on launch would leave the child with no access to
any level. Starting again from a new player state keeps every level available.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_OfflineUse}, \defref{def_Player}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Reliability}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ReliableSaveData}{20}{SaveDataCorruption}{2-18}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Rewards and Scoreboard}
\label{sec_FRRewards}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_FirstReward}]
When a level is finished for the first time, the system shall give its level
reward. The reward is the level's journey points, together with its coins and
its accessory if the level's Reward Definition includes them. The system shall then display a reward screen
showing what was earned.

\textit{Rationale:} Rewards are for reaching the end of a level, not for
correct answers. A child who needed hints receives the same reward, so no
child is left behind. Defining the reward per level, as coins, an accessory,
both, or neither, lets the team tune the rewards without changing the
requirement.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_CompleteGame}, \defref{def_Ops},
\thmref{thm_Once}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Offline}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{NonPunitiveFeedback}{12}{PunitiveFeedback}{2-12}.

\item[\frdef{FR_NoDuplicateReward}]
When a level whose reward has already been given is finished again, the system
shall congratulate the child without giving points, coins, or accessories.

\textit{Rationale:} Giving the same reward again would encourage the child to
replay one level instead of exploring the journey, and would make the totals
on the scoreboard meaningless.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_CompleteGame}, \thmref{thm_Once}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Scoreboard}]
The system shall display a personal scoreboard. For each level, it shall show
the name, the status, and the points earned. It shall also show the values of
\defref{def_Scoreboard}: the total journey points, the number of completed
levels out of the total, and the coin balance. It shall show the selected
character and the nickname if they are set. It shall not compare the child
with other users.

\textit{Rationale:} The scoreboard shows the child what they have achieved.
Comparing children would add pressure, which works against the goal of
reducing anxiety.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ViewProgress}, \defref{def_Scoreboard}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Characters and Accessories}
\label{sec_FRCharacters}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_ChooseCharacter}]
The system shall display every character the child can choose. When the child
confirms a character, the system shall select it. Choosing a character shall
change only the selected character.

\textit{Rationale:} Choosing a character gives the child a sense of ownership
of the journey. Changing characters must not cost the child any progress or
rewards.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ChooseCharacter}.

\item[\frdef{FR_DiverseCharacters}]
The characters shall vary in skin tone, hair texture and style, body type,
and gender expression. They shall include options with accessibility and
medical devices, such as glasses, an eye patch, or a wheelchair. The
accessories shall include items from a range of cultures and religions.

\textit{Rationale:} A child who can see themselves in a character feels that the game, and
the clinic it prepares them for, is a place where they belong.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ChooseCharacter}, \ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory},
\ucref{UC_EquipAccessory}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Inclusivity}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{CharacterDiversityInclusivity}{15}{DiverseCharacterLibrary}{2-9}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Shop}]
The system shall display a shop of every accessory that has a price. Each accessory shall show a preview on the
selected character, its price, and whether the child owns it.

\textit{Rationale:} Seeing a preview and the price lets the child decide what
to save coins for. Marking owned accessories prevents the child from trying to
buy something twice.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Purchase}]
When the child confirms the purchase of an accessory that is not owned and
costs at most the coin balance, the system shall subtract the price from the
coin balance and add the accessory to the owned accessories.

\textit{Rationale:} Buying turns the coins the child earned into a visible
reward, which gives the child a reason to continue the journey.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Offline}.

\item[\frdef{FR_PurchaseRefused}]
When the child tries to buy an accessory that is already owned or costs more
than the coin balance, the system shall not change the player state. If the
accessory is owned, the system shall offer to equip it. If it costs too much,
the system shall show how many more coins are needed.

\textit{Rationale:} Telling the child why the purchase did not happen avoids
confusion. Not changing anything means the coin balance can never become
negative.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Equip}]
The system shall allow the child to equip any owned accessory and to unequip
any equipped accessory. At most one accessory of each category shall be
equipped at a time. Equipping or unequipping shall not remove any
accessory from the owned accessories.

\textit{Rationale:} One accessory per category keeps the character's
appearance sensible, for example one hat at a time. Keeping owned accessories
means the child never loses something they earned.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_EquipAccessory}.

\item[\frdef{FR_NoRealMoney}]
The system shall not offer any purchase with real money.

\textit{Rationale:} Coins are earned only by playing. Real-money purchases
would not be appropriate for young children and would conflict with app store
policies for children's applications.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_PurchaseAccessory}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_StoreCompliance}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsubsection{Caregiver Features}
\label{sec_FRCaregiver}

\begin{reqlist}{FR}

\item[\frdef{FR_ParentGate}]
The system shall place the settings behind a parental gate, which is a
task that an adult can complete but a child aged 3 to 7 is unlikely to
complete. The child area shall not offer any setting.

\textit{Rationale:} Settings such as the audio and the visual cues decide whether the child
can follow a level. A child who changed them by accident could be left unable
to continue. App store policies for children's applications also expect a
parental gate in front of adult functions.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_AdjustPreferences}, \ucref{UC_DeleteData}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_StoreCompliance}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{VisualCuesSetting}{19}{VisualCuesDisabledSetting}{2-15}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ProcedureGuide}]
The system shall provide a caregiver procedure guide. For each procedure, the
guide shall explain what the child may experience and how the matching level
presents it.

\textit{Rationale:} Many caregivers do not know what happens during these
procedures. The guide helps them answer the child's questions and support the
child at the appointment.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_ProcedureInformation}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Language}, \nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Nickname}]
The settings shall allow the caregiver to enter, change, or clear an optional
nickname. When no nickname is set, the system shall use wording that is not
personalized.

\textit{Rationale:} A nickname makes the journey feel like the child's own. It
is optional, and it does not need to be the child's real name, so the
application does not need to collect identifying information.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_AdjustPreferences}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_DataProtection}, \nfrref{NFR_Minimization}.

\item[\frdef{FR_Preferences}]
The settings shall allow the caregiver to change the age group and the audio,
display, and accessibility preferences. Each change shall take effect
immediately.

\textit{Rationale:} Caregivers know what their child needs, such as a lower
volume in a waiting room. Applying changes immediately lets the caregiver see
the result before handing the device back.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_AdjustPreferences}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{SafeAudioVisuals}{14}{UnsafeAudioVisuals}{2-16}, \hazsr{VisualCuesSetting}{19}{VisualCuesDisabledSetting}{2-15}.

\item[\frdef{FR_AgeGroup}]
When an age group is set and a level provides content for that age group, the
system shall present that content.

\textit{Rationale:} A three-year-old and a seven-year-old understand
explanations differently. Content for the child's age group is easier to
follow.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_AdjustPreferences}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ResetJourney}]
The settings shall allow the caregiver to reset the journey data. The system
shall first state what will be erased and ask for confirmation. On
confirmation, the system shall apply $\mathit{reset}$ (\defref{def_Ops}),
which returns the player state to the new player state
(\defref{def_Player}). It shall also remove all accessories, deselect the
character, and clear the nickname. The notice acknowledgement shall be kept. On cancellation, no data
shall change.

\textit{Rationale:} A reset lets a family start over, or pass the device to
another child. The confirmation prevents accidental loss of progress. The
acknowledgement is kept because the same caregiver has already read the
notice.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_DeleteData}, \defref{def_Player},
\defref{def_Ops}, \thmref{thm_StatusMachine}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Minimization}.

\item[\frdef{FR_ClearAllData}]
The settings shall allow the caregiver to clear all local application data
after confirmation. This shall erase the data of \rref{FR_ResetJourney} and the
notice acknowledgement, so that the medical notice is shown again on the next
launch.

\textit{Rationale:} A caregiver may want to remove everything the application
has stored, for example before giving the device to someone else.

\textit{Traces:} \ucref{UC_DeleteData}.

\textit{Related NFRs:} \nfrref{NFR_Minimization}.

\end{reqlist}

\section{Data Requirements}
\label{sec_Data_Requirements}

This section describes the information managed by \progname{}
to support \termmark{journeybasedprogression}journey based progression, character customization,
game completion tracking, rewards, and a personal scoreboard.
The application does not require patient accounts or
integration with clinical information systems. All user
data is maintained locally on the user's device.

Each ophthalmology procedure is represented by one
preparation game, which acts as a level in the child's
journey. Games may contain multiple smaller interactions
or rounds, but these are treated as part of the game
rather than as separate data entities.

All levels remain accessible regardless of whether previous
levels have been completed. Children can earn journey points,
receive in game coins or cosmetic accessories, and use
their rewards to customize a selected character.

Journey points accumulate across completed games and are
displayed on the personal scoreboard. In game coins are
tracked separately and may be spent on cosmetic accessories.
Rewards are granted upon the first completion of each game,
including when individual exercises have been skipped. A reward
may include in game coins, a cosmetic accessory, both, or neither.


\subsection{Major Data Entities}

The system manages the following major data entities:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Procedure Game:}
    Represents a game designed to familiarize children
    with a particular ophthalmology procedure, such as
    vision testing, eye-drop administration, or slit-lamp
    examinations. Each procedure has one corresponding
    game that represents a level in the child's journey.
    A game may contain multiple interactive exercises
    or rounds, and its content may vary according to
    the child's age group.

    \item \textbf{Character:}
    Represents a character available for the child to
    select and use throughout the preparation journey.
    Each character has a unique identifier, a display
    name, and a visual representation.

    \item \textbf{Cosmetic Accessory:}
    Represents a virtual item, such as a hat or glasses,
    used to customize a character's appearance.
    Accessories may be purchased using in game coins
    or received directly as game completion rewards.
    Cosmetic accessories do not affect gameplay
    performance or clinical preparation activities.

    \item \textbf{\termmark{characterinventory}Character Inventory:}
    Represents the cosmetic accessories owned by the
    child and identifies which accessories are currently
    equipped on the selected character. Owned items
    remain available when the child changes characters.

    \item \textbf{Reward Definition:}
    Represents the predefined reward associated with
    the first completion of a procedure game.
    Each reward includes journey points, and may also
    include in-game coins, a cosmetic accessory, both,
    or neither.
    Rewards are based on game completion rather
    than the accuracy of individual responses.

    \item \textbf{Local Preferences:}
    Represents locally stored application settings,
    including the child's optional nickname, selected
    character, age group, display or accessibility
    preferences, and caregiver acknowledgment of
    the application's educational purpose.
    These settings do not require a patient account.

    \item \textbf{Game Progress:}
    Represents the completion status, journey points
    earned, and reward claim status for each preparation
    game. Progress is tracked at the game level rather
    than for individual rounds or exercises.
    This information reflects participation in the
    application rather than clinical performance
    or assessment results.

    \item \textbf{Journey Progress:}
    Represents the child's overall progression across
    all preparation games, including accumulated journey
    points, completed levels, and the current in game
    coin balance. Total journey points and completed
    levels are calculated from Game Progress records,
    while the spendable coin balance is maintained
    locally and updated when coins are earned or spent.

\end{itemize}

The relationship between these entities is as follows:
each Procedure Game represents one ophthalmology procedure
and corresponds to one level in the journey. Each game has
an associated Reward Definition and a Game Progress record.

Local Preferences identifies the character selected by the
child, while Character Inventory tracks the Cosmetic
Accessories owned and equipped by the child.

When a game is completed for the first time, the system
records its completion, awards the corresponding journey
points, and grants any coins and cosmetic accessory in
its Reward Definition.
Game Progress records whether the reward has been claimed
to prevent duplicate rewards when a completed game is
replayed.

Journey Progress summarizes individual Game Progress
records to calculate accumulated journey points and
completed levels. The coin balance is maintained
separately because coins may be spent in the
character customization shop.

Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec} specifies formally how
level progress and rewards change during play.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}

\progname{} follows a \termmark{dataminimization}data-minimization approach,
collecting and retaining only the information necessary
to support character customization, journey progression,
rewards, user preferences, and local game progress.
The application's privacy and data management
practices are outlined below:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Data Collection:}
    The application does not require patient accounts,
    medical records, appointment details, or \termmark{personallyidentifiableinformationpii}personally
    identifying information. Optional nicknames are
    used solely for personalization and do not need
    to correspond to the child's legal name.

    \item \textbf{Character Selection and Customization:}
    The application stores the selected character,
    owned cosmetic accessories, and equipped accessories
    locally. Character customization does not require
    registration or the collection of personal information.

    \item \textbf{Game and Journey Progress:}
    The application stores completion status, journey
    points, and reward claim status for each preparation
    game. Total points and completed levels are calculated
    from these records and displayed on the personal
    scoreboard. This information does not represent
    clinical performance or visual ability.

    \item \textbf{Rewards and Virtual Currency:}
    In-game coins and cosmetic accessories are earned
    through game completion. Coins may be exchanged
    for accessories within the application, while
    some accessories are awarded directly.
    Coin balances and owned accessories are stored
    locally. No real-money purchases or external
    payment services are required.

    \item \textbf{Data Storage and Protection:}
    User preferences, character selections, game
    progress, coin balances, and accessory inventories
    shall be stored locally on the user's device
    and protected from unauthorized access by other
    applications. The application shall not transmit
    this information to external servers or require
    user accounts.

    \item \textbf{Data Retention and Deletion:}
    Locally stored information is retained until
    deleted by the caregiver or through application
    removal, subject to the device's backup and
    restoration behaviour. Caregivers can clear
    stored preferences, character selections,
    game progress, points, coin balances, and
    accessory inventories through the application's
    data management functionality.

    \item \textbf{Consent and Access:}
    The application does not require registration
    or the collection of personal information
    to access its preparation games. Caregivers
    acknowledge the application's educational
    purpose during initial setup. All games remain
    available regardless of whether previous
    levels have been completed.

    \item \textbf{Scoreboard Privacy:}
    The personal scoreboard displays only the child's
    accumulated journey points, completed levels,
    and current coin balance. It does not compare
    scores with other children or require an
    online leaderboard.

    \item \textbf{Offline Functionality:}
    Procedure games, characters, cosmetic accessories,
    and reward definitions are available locally.
    Game progress, rewards, accessory purchases,
    and character customization do not require
    an Internet connection or external server.

    \item \textbf{Research and Regulatory Compliance:}
    Any personal information collected separately
    for research or usability evaluation is outside
    the application's operational data storage
    and is subject to applicable institutional
    ethics and privacy requirements.

\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\label{non-func-req}
% ==============================================================

The following non-functional requirements define the quality attributes
and constraints of the application. Each requirement includes a rationale
and a measurable fit criterion. Each requirement may also include a \textit{Traces} line that lists
the functional requirements that help satisfy it. A requirement that comes from
the \hadoc{Hazard Analysis} also has a \textit{Hazard Analysis} line, in the
same form as in Section~\ref{sec_FRList}.

\subsection{Look and Feel Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Layout}] Interactive controls that serve the same function shall remain in consistent screen positions across all screens where they appear.

\textit{Rationale:} Consistent control placement helps children locate and recognize controls without relying on text labels. Maintaining consistent positions across different activities and game features reduces confusion and supports independent navigation.

\textit{\termmark{fitcriterion}Fit Criterion:} A design review confirms that controls serving the same function remain within \constref{CONTROL_POSITION_TOLERANCE_MM} millimetres of their designated positions across all applicable screens.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_ChildSafe}]
No image, animation, narration, or sound shall be frightening, or show pain or distress beyond the sensations in the approved script.

\textit{Rationale:} The clinic is already unfamiliar to the child, so the application must not add to the fear it is meant to reduce. Child-life specialists know what frightens children of this age, and the team does not.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} A child-life specialist reviews all content against a checklist, and no finding is unresolved at release.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_DescribeSensations}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ChildLifeReview}{9}{Frightening}{2-7}, \hazsr{ThirdPartyAssetReview}{23}{ThirdPartyAssetContent}{2-21}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Usability and Humanity Requirements} \label{sec_UsabilityReqs}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_TouchTarget}]
Every interactive element shall be at least \constref{TOUCH_TARGET_MIN} millimetres wide and high on screen.

\textit{Rationale:} The Apple and Android guidance minimums are about 7 mm and 9 mm \cite{AppleHIG, MaterialDesign}. Children aged 3 to 7 have less precise motor control, so a larger size is used. The value is to be confirmed in usability testing.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Measured on each reference device, every interactive element meets this size.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AccessibleInteraction}{24}{InaccessibleInteraction}{2-3}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Exit}]
A control that returns to the home menu shall be reachable with one touch from every screen of an activity.

\textit{Rationale:} A child or parent who chooses the wrong activity, or wants to stop, needs a way out that does not depend on reading. Without one, the child may feel trapped, which works against the goal of reassurance.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} On every screen of every activity, a tester reaches the home menu with one touch.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_ExitLevel}, \rref{FR_StopOnExit}, \rref{FR_RestartLevel}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}, \hazsr{StopAudioVisualsOnExit}{13}{AudioVisualsContinueOnExit}{2-13}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Usability}] A first-time user from the defined user characteristics shall be able to use the application's core features without reading instructions or receiving prior training.

\textit{Rationale:} The application is intended for use at home with little or no assistance. Users should be able to understand and interact with its core features independently, supporting engagement and reducing reliance on parental guidance.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} In usability testing, at least \constref{UNAIDED_RATE_MIN}\% of participants shall successfully complete a predefined core task without verbal assistance, with a median completion time of at most \constref{ACTIVITY_PAIR_MAX_MIN} minutes. Participants aged 3--4 may have a parent present who does not explain or guide the task.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_JourneyMap}, \rref{FR_SpokenInstructions}, \rref{FR_HintOnMiss}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AccessibleInteraction}{24}{InaccessibleInteraction}{2-3}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Language}]
Every narrated sentence shall have at most \constref{SENTENCE_MAX_WORDS} words and use words familiar to a child aged 3 to 7, and text in the adult area shall be at or below Grade \constref{ADULT_TEXT_MAX_GRADE} reading level.

\textit{Rationale:} Young children follow short sentences in familiar words, and long ones are lost. Adults vary in reading ability, and Grade 8 matches the defined user characteristics. The sentence limit is to be confirmed with the child-life specialists.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} A count over all scripts finds no narrated sentence over \constref{SENTENCE_MAX_WORDS} words, a child-life specialist finds no unfamiliar word, and a readability formula gives a grade of at most \constref{ADULT_TEXT_MAX_GRADE} for each adult-area text.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_SpokenInstructions}, \rref{FR_ProcedureGuide}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AccessibleInteraction}{24}{InaccessibleInteraction}{2-3}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Accessibility}]
The application shall meet the \termmark{webcontentaccessibilityguidelineswcag}WCAG 2.2 Level AA success criteria that apply to a native mobile application.

\textit{Rationale:} Children who attend an eye clinic may have low vision, and some will have hearing or motor differences. WCAG is the accepted benchmark \cite{WCAG22}, and the same duty applies in Ontario \cite{AODA2005}.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} An accessibility audit against the applicable criteria finds no failure.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_SpokenInstructions}, \rref{FR_VisualCues}, \rref{FR_NarrationFallback}, \rref{FR_RevealAnswer}, \rref{FR_Preferences}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{AccessibleInteraction}{24}{InaccessibleInteraction}{2-3}, \hazsr{NarrationMuted}{18}{NarrationDisabledSetting}{2-14}, \hazsr{VisualCuesSetting}{19}{VisualCuesDisabledSetting}{2-15}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Colour}]
Every state and prompt shall remain distinguishable without colour, and for users with common forms of colour vision deficiency.

\textit{Rationale:} Colour vision deficiency is common enough that some children and parents will have it, and it is more likely among users of an eye service. Relying on colour alone, for example green for correct and red for wrong, would hide information from them.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Screens viewed under protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia simulation show every state and choice as distinct by shape, position, or text as well as colour.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_JourneyMap}, \rref{FR_RevealAnswer}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{CorrectAnswerReveal}{16}{CorrectAnswerReveal}{2-10}, \hazsr{AccessibleInteraction}{24}{InaccessibleInteraction}{2-3}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Performance Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Startup}]
The home menu shall appear within \constref{STARTUP_MAX_S} seconds of launch.

\textit{Rationale:} A child looking at a blank screen may lose interest before anything starts. \termmark{lowspecdevice}Low-spec devices set the limit, and the value is revisited after proof-of-concept measurements.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The median of \constref{STARTUP_TRIALS_MIN} cold starts on each low-spec device is at most \constref{STARTUP_MAX_S} seconds.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Responsiveness}]
The system shall respond visibly to a touch on an interactive element within \constref{RESPONSE_MAX_MS} milliseconds.

\textit{Rationale:} About 0.1 seconds is the longest delay that feels instant to a user \cite{Nielsen1993}. A child who sees no reaction will tap again, which the activities then have to handle.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} At least \constref{RESPONSE_RATE_MIN}\% of measured touches on each low-spec device receive a visible response within \constref{RESPONSE_MAX_MS} milliseconds.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_IgnoreRepeatedTouches}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{DevicePerformance}{2}{PoorPerformance}{1-2}, \hazsr{PreventAccidentalInput}{3}{AccidentalInputSkipping}{2-1}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Smoothness}]
Animation shall run at no fewer than \constref{FRAME_RATE_MIN} frames per second.

\textit{Rationale:} Choppy animation of a procedure would distract from what it shows.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} On each low-spec device, the frame rate is at least \constref{FRAME_RATE_MIN} frames per second for at least \constref{FRAME_RATE_COMPLIANCE_MIN}\% of the time over a full walkthrough and practice activity.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{DevicePerformance}{2}{PoorPerformance}{1-2}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_AVSync}]
Narration shall start within \constref{AV_SYNC_MAX_MS} milliseconds of the animation it accompanies.

\textit{Rationale:} Narration that does not match the picture can teach the wrong step. The value is a practical limit that keeps the two looking together, and it is checked in the proof of concept.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Measured on each reference device across all steps, no offset exceeds this value.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_SpokenInstructions}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ConsistentAudioVisuals}{25}{AudioVisualMismatch}{2-17}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_AudioSafety}]
No sound effect or music shall have a peak level above the peak level of the narration.

\textit{Rationale:} A sudden loud sound can frighten a child who is already anxious, and can be uncomfortable for small ears. Tying every sound to the narration level, which the parent sets with the device volume, avoids a sound that is louder than the voice.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} An audio analysis of all sound assets finds no exception.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_NarrationFallback}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{SafeAudioVisuals}{14}{UnsafeAudioVisuals}{2-16}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Reliability}]
The application shall not crash or freeze during use.

\textit{Rationale:} A crash in front of an anxious child, or in a waiting room minutes before an appointment, harms trust in the application.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} At least \constref{CRASH_FREE_RATE_MIN}\% of at least \constref{TEST_SESSIONS_MIN} test sessions end without a crash or freeze.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_RestartLevel}, \rref{FR_Suspend}, \rref{FR_PersistState}, \rref{FR_CorruptData}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{GameplayRecovery}{4}{GameplayStuck}{2-2}, \hazsr{ReliableSaveData}{20}{SaveDataCorruption}{2-18}, \hazsr{ThirdPartyValidation}{22}{ThirdPartyFailure}{2-20}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_DeviceHeat}]
The application shall not cause the device temperature to exceed \constref{DEVICE_TEMP_MAX_C} degrees Celsius during use.

\textit{Rationale:} Young children may hold a device for long periods, and a device that becomes too hot can cause discomfort or minor injury. The limit is revisited after proof-of-concept measurements on low-spec devices.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} On each low-spec device, the device temperature stays at or below this value over a continuous session of at least \constref{HEAT_TEST_SESSION_MIN} minutes of gameplay.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{DeviceResourceUse}{1}{DeviceOverheating}{1-1}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Operational and Environmental Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Offline}]
After installation, every function shall work with no network connection.

\textit{Rationale:} Hospital waiting rooms often have poor connectivity, and that is a place where the application is useful. A network dependency would also raise privacy questions.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The full functional test suite passes with the device in airplane mode.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_PersistState}, \rref{FR_FirstReward}, \rref{FR_Purchase}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{OfflinePreparation}{26}{OfflineContentUnavailable}{2-4}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Portability}]
The application shall run on iOS \constref{MIN_IOS} and later and Android \constref{MIN_ANDROID} and later, on phones and tablets with screens from \constref{SCREEN_MIN_IN} to \constref{SCREEN_MAX_IN} inches.

\textit{Rationale:} Families use the devices they already own. These versions cover the large majority of devices in use by families and keep the supported range testable by a small team. A low-spec device is one released at least \constref{LOW_SPEC_AGE_YEARS} years before the test date that still supports the minimum version. The values are revisited after the proof of concept.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The functional tests in the \termmark{verificationandvalidationvnvplan}VnV Plan pass on every reference device.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Installability}]
A parent shall be able to install the application from the app store and reach the home menu without creating an account, granting permissions, or downloading further content.

\textit{Rationale:} Every extra step is a place where a parent may give up, and a required account would conflict with the decision to collect no personal data.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} On each reference device, a tester installs the application and reaches the home menu following only the store listing, with none of those steps required.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{OfflinePreparation}{26}{OfflineContentUnavailable}{2-4}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_InstallSize}]
The installed size of the application shall be at most \constref{INSTALL_SIZE_MAX_MB} megabytes.

\textit{Rationale:} The limit is chosen so a family can download over a mobile connection and so older devices with little storage can hold it. The proof of concept measures the real size.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The size reported by each app store for the release build is within this limit.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Maintainability and Support Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Maintainability}]
The effort to make any likely change in the Likely Changes section shall be less than \constref{MAINT_FRACTION} of the original development time, not counting clinical review.

\textit{Rationale:} A change such as a new procedure should cost far less than building the first one, or the application cannot keep up with what clinicians want covered.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The recorded effort for each such change made during the project is within this fraction.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Security Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_DataProtection}]
Stored settings and progress shall be readable only by the application, using the storage the platform keeps private to it.

\textit{Rationale:} The nickname and progress are about a child, and no other application or person using the device should read them. The platform already provides private storage.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} On an unmodified test device, no other application and no file-sharing function can read the stored data.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_PersistState}, \rref{FR_Nickname}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MinimalSaveData}{21}{SaveDataPrivacy}{2-19}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Integrity}]
Every release build shall be digitally signed and distributed only through the Apple App Store and Google Play Store.

\textit{Rationale:} What children see must be what clinicians approved. A signed build from the official stores means the content cannot have been altered on the way to the device.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The signature of each release build verifies with the team's signing key using the platform tools, and no other distribution channel is in use.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ClinicianApproval}{5}{InaccurateProcedure}{2-5}, \hazsr{ApprovedContent}{7}{InaccurateProcedure}{2-5}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Privacy}]
The application shall not transmit any data off the device.

\textit{Rationale:} Not collecting data removes most of the privacy and legal exposure for a service aimed at children, who cannot consent, and the application does not need any data to work. Evaluation measures are collected separately, with consent.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} A network capture during a full test session shows no outbound request from the application.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MinimalSaveData}{21}{UnexpectedDataTransmission}{2-22}, \hazsr{ThirdPartyValidation}{22}{UnexpectedDataTransmission}{2-22}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Minimization}]
The application shall store only the nickname, age band, display settings, and progress record.

\textit{Rationale:} Data that is not stored cannot leak or be requested. Only these items are needed for the application to function.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Inspection of the storage after a full test session finds only these items.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_Nickname}, \rref{FR_ResetJourney}, \rref{FR_ClearAllData}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MinimalSaveData}{21}{SaveDataPrivacy}{2-19}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Immunity}]
Each release shall contain no third-party library with a publicly known vulnerability of high or critical severity.

\textit{Rationale:} The application has no server and accepts no input except touch, so its main exposure is through the libraries it includes. A compromised dependency could break the privacy requirements.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} A dependency vulnerability scan run on each release build reports none of that severity.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ThirdPartyValidation}{22}{UnexpectedDataTransmission}{2-22}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_DependencyTesting}]
Every third-party library and engine component shall be fixed to an exact version, and each change to one shall be tested on a release build before it is included in a release.

\textit{Rationale:} The application depends on software the team does not control, and an unexpected update or incompatibility could cause crashes or wrong behaviour in the activities that prepare the child. Fixing versions means each release contains only what was tested.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The dependency list gives an exact version for every third-party library and engine component, and the functional test suite passes on the release build after each dependency change, with no finding unresolved at release.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ThirdPartyValidation}{22}{ThirdPartyFailure}{2-20}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Cultural and Political Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Inclusivity}]
Content shall not assume a child's family structure, culture, religion, gender, or ethnicity.

\textit{Rationale:} Families using the service come from many backgrounds, and a child who does not see themselves in the content, or is shown a family unlike theirs, may feel left out when the aim is reassurance.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The review of child-facing content includes an inclusivity checklist, and no finding is unresolved at release.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_DiverseCharacters}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{CharacterDiversityInclusivity}{15}{DiverseCharacterLibrary}{2-9}.

\end{reqlist}

\subsection{Legal Requirements}

\begin{reqlist}{NFR}

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_NoClaims}]
The application, its notices, and its store listings shall make no claim to diagnose, screen, treat, or monitor any condition.

\textit{Rationale:} Software that makes such claims may be regulated as a medical device \cite{HealthCanadaSaMD}. Such a claim would also encourage a parent to treat the practice as a vision test and miss a real problem.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Two team members and a clinician review every child-facing text, adult-facing text, and store listing against a checklist, and find no such claim.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_MedicalNotice}, \rref{FR_ProcedureGuide}.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{MedicalDisclaimer}{10}{MisinterpretedMedicalPurpose}{2-8}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_Licensing}]
Every third-party asset and library shall have a recorded licence that permits redistribution under the project's chosen software licence.

\textit{Rationale:} Assets from a public store may forbid redistribution or require terms that conflict with the project's chosen licence. A removal for a licence breach would take the application away from families.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} The licence log lists every third-party asset and library with its licence, and has no gap.

\textit{Hazard Analysis:} \hazsr{ThirdPartyAssetReview}{23}{ThirdPartyAssetContent}{2-21}.

\item[\nfrdef{NFR_StoreCompliance}]
The application shall meet the Apple and Google policies for children's applications listed in the project's standards table.

\textit{Rationale:} The application is aimed at children, and the policies call for parental gates and limit advertising, outside links, and third-party data collection \cite{AppleGuidelines, GooglePlayPolicy}. Non-compliance would lead to rejection or removal.

\textit{Fit Criterion:} Both app stores approve the release build for a children's audience, and every item on the pre-submission policy checklist passes.

\textit{Traces:} \rref{FR_NoRealMoney}, \rref{FR_ParentGate}.

\end{reqlist}

\section{Rationale}
\label{rationale}

\subsection{Scope}

The scope is focused on preparing children aged 3 to 7 for common paediatric
ophthalmology procedures. Diagnosis, treatment, and other clinical functions
were excluded because they are outside the main goal of the application and
would add requirements that are not needed for preparation. The initial set
of procedures is also limited so that the team can focus on making each
activity clear, accessible, and reliable. Features such as AR and personalized
preparation are considered stretch goals rather than part of the minimum
scope.

\subsection{Functional Requirements}

Each functional requirement has its own rationale in Section~\ref{sec_FRList}.
This subsection explains only the decisions that shape most of the
requirements.

The requirements follow the journey design recommended by the project
supervisor (see the Data Requirements rationale below). The procedures needed
at an appointment can change at short notice, so every level is open at all
times and progress is recorded per level. The journey map, the rewards, the
scoreboard, and the characters all build on this structure.

The format of each level is not yet decided. The requirements therefore state
what every level must convey and how the journey records progress, but not
what happens inside a level. Requirements for the inside of each level will be
added once the format is decided.

The application must not become a new source of stress for a child who is
already anxious. The application therefore has no failure states. Mistakes
lead to hints, exercises can be skipped, rewards do not depend on answers, and
the child can leave at any time without losing anything. Wherever a
requirement could cost the child progress, it is written so that it does not.

The users are children aged 3 to 7, most of whom cannot read. Every
instruction is therefore given through narration and pictures, and nothing in
the child area depends on reading. Everything that is not play, such as the
medical notice, the settings, and deleting data, is placed in the caregiver
area.

The application works offline and stores only the player state on the device.
No account, network, or personal information is needed. The requirements on
saving, restoring, and resetting data all follow from this decision.

The Hazard Analysis states its safety and security requirements at a general
level. This SRS states them as specific, testable requirements. Those that
describe what the system does are functional requirements, and each one names
the safety and security requirement and the hazard it addresses. Those that
describe a quality of the system or the release process are non-functional
requirements.

The journey and the player's progress are specified formally in
Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec}. They involve state over time, such as rewards
given, levels started, and coins earned. Informal wording such as ``the reward
is given once'' is easy to misread for this kind of behaviour. The player
state is specified with discrete mathematics because it is a small set of
values with clear operations. The status of each level is also shown as a
state machine (\thmref{thm_StatusMachine}), because it is easiest to check as
a picture. Every requirement can then cite exactly the part of the model it
depends on.

\subsection{Data Requirements}

The data requirements were developed by considering how
ophthalmology procedures and their preparation games should
be represented. Initially, the team considered a one to many
relationship where each procedure could have multiple
preparation activities. However, since the team had not established
whether multiple distinct games would be developed for
each procedure, a simpler one to one relationship was selected.
Each procedure is represented by one game, which may contain
multiple smaller exercises or rounds.

The team also considered two approaches for selecting preparation
games: allowing caregivers to manually choose games based
on clinical recommendations, or having caregivers enter a
clinician provided code to automatically select the appropriate
games. The team consulted the project supervisor to determine
which approach would be more practical in a clinical setting.

The supervisor recommended making all games accessible,
since the procedures required during an examination may
change unexpectedly. The supervisor also suggested presenting the
games as a journey with levels, rewards, a personal
scoreboard, and character customization.

Based on this feedback, the team removed the caregiver configuration
and recommendation code entities from the data requirements.
Instead, entities supporting character selection, 
game progress, journey progression, and rewards
were introduced. All user data is stored locally to maintain
offline functionality and minimize the collection of
personal information.

Rewards are defined flexibly. A level reward may include coins, a
cosmetic accessory, both, or neither, so that the team and the
supervisors can tune the rewards without changing the data model.

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}

This section explains decisions that affect multiple non-functional requirements. Individual
requirement rationales are provided alongside their respective requirements.

The main non-functional requirements support three priorities: child-friendly
interaction, reliable use, and privacy. Since the application is designed for
children aged 3 to 7, requirements such as \nfrref{NFR_TouchTarget},
\nfrref{NFR_Layout}, and \nfrref{NFR_Usability} ensure that activities are
easy to navigate and complete. \nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe} and
\nfrref{NFR_Language} ensure that the content is appropriate for the target
age group.

Accessibility is also important so that children with different needs can
use the application. \nfrref{NFR_Accessibility} and \nfrref{NFR_Colour}
address accessibility needs, while \nfrref{NFR_AVSync} and
\nfrref{NFR_AudioSafety} help keep audio and animations clear during
activities.

Since the application may be used at home or before an appointment,
\nfrref{NFR_Offline} allows activities to work without internet access.
\nfrref{NFR_Responsiveness}, \nfrref{NFR_Smoothness}, and
\nfrref{NFR_Reliability} help prevent delays and interruptions that could
affect the experience. \nfrref{NFR_Portability} and
\nfrref{NFR_Installability} also make the application easier to access
across supported devices.

Finally, privacy and security are especially important because the
application is intended for children. \nfrref{NFR_Minimization} limits
the information stored, \nfrref{NFR_Privacy} prevents data from being
transmitted off the device, and \nfrref{NFR_DataProtection} protects
locally stored data. \nfrref{NFR_Integrity} and \nfrref{NFR_Immunity}
help protect the application from unauthorized changes and vulnerable
dependencies. Other requirements, including \nfrref{NFR_Inclusivity},
\nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}, \nfrref{NFR_Licensing}, and
\nfrref{NFR_StoreCompliance}, address inclusive content, the application's
medical scope, and distribution requirements.

\section{Likely Changes}
\label{sec_LikelyChanges}

The following changes are expected during the project or after release. The
design should make each of them easy to carry out
(\nfrref{NFR_Maintainability}). Each change lists the parts of this document
it affects.

\begin{reqlist}{LC}

\item[\lcdef{LC_LevelFormat}]
The format of each level. The activities inside a level are not yet decided,
and they will be chosen after prototyping and feedback from the supervisor.

\textit{Affects:} \rref{FR_LevelContent},
Sections~\ref{sec_FRGameplay} and~\ref{sec_FRInstructions}. The formal model
(Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec}) is not affected because it does not describe
the inside of a level.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Procedures}]
The set of procedures. Levels may be added, removed, or reordered as the
supervisor confirms which procedures are most common.

\textit{Affects:} the game content (Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec}), the data
dictionary (Table~\ref{tab:JourneyDataDictionary}), the procedure guide
(\rref{FR_ProcedureGuide}), and the level use cases. The requirements and the
formal model hold for any number of levels.

\item[\lcdef{LC_ClinicalContent}]
The procedure content. The wording, images, step order, and sensation
descriptions of a level may change after clinical and child-life review.

\textit{Affects:} the content of each level and of the procedure guide
(\rref{FR_DescribeSensations}, \rref{FR_ProcedureGuide}).

\item[\lcdef{LC_Rewards}]
The rewards. The points, coins, and accessory given by each level, and the
price of each accessory, will be tuned during testing.

\textit{Affects:} the game content (Section~\ref{sec_FormalSpec}) and the
Reward Definition entity. A reward may already include coins, an accessory,
both, or neither, so no requirement changes (\rref{FR_FirstReward}).

\item[\lcdef{LC_Characters}]
The characters, accessories, and accessory categories offered.

\textit{Affects:} \rref{FR_ChooseCharacter}, \rref{FR_DiverseCharacters}, and \rref{FR_Shop}.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Constants}]
The values of the symbolic constants, after usability testing, performance
measurements, and clinician feedback.

\textit{Affects:} Table~\ref{tab:Constants} only. Requirements refer to the
constants by name.

\item[\lcdef{LC_AgeContent}]
Content for each age group. Levels may add separate content for younger and
older children.

\textit{Affects:} \rref{FR_AgeGroup} and the content of each level.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Language}]
Additional languages, most likely French for families in Ontario.

\textit{Affects:} the narration and text of every level, the procedure guide,
and \nfrref{NFR_Language}.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Platforms}]
The supported operating system versions and screen sizes.

\textit{Affects:} \nfrref{NFR_Portability} and \conref{C_MobilePlatforms}.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Hazards}]
The safety and security requirements. The Hazard Analysis will be revised as
the design develops, and it may add, remove, or renumber requirements.

\textit{Affects:} the requirements that have a Hazard Analysis line, and
Table~\ref{tab:HA2FRtm}.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Assets}]
The final art and audio, which will replace the placeholder assets used during
development.

\textit{Affects:} the content only. The assets must still meet
\nfrref{NFR_ChildSafe} and \nfrref{NFR_InstallSize}.

\item[\lcdef{LC_AR}]
Augmented reality presentation of procedures, which is a stretch goal. It
would require access to the camera.

\textit{Affects:} the level requirements, \nfrref{NFR_Installability}, and the
Hazard Analysis.

\item[\lcdef{LC_Personalization}]
Personalized preparation based on the child's appointment, which is a stretch
goal.

\textit{Affects:} the journey requirements (Section~\ref{sec_FRJourney}), the
caregiver settings (Section~\ref{sec_FRCaregiver}), and the data requirements
(Section~\ref{sec_Data_Requirements}).

\item[\lcdef{LC_Stakeholders}]
The stakeholder requirements can expand once more interviews and elicitation is conducted with the 
primary stakeholders (children, caregivers) after ethics board approval. The stakeholder requirements 
will also be revised as the design develops, and it may add, remove, or renumber requirements.

\textit{Affects:} Stakeholder Requirements in Section~\ref{sec_StakeholderReqs}.

\end{reqlist}

\section{Unlikely Changes}
\label{sec_UnlikelyChanges}

The design can assume that the following will not change. Each one is stated
with the reason it is considered stable.

\begin{reqlist}{ULC}

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_Users}]
The primary users are children aged 3 to 7 who are preparing for a paediatric
eye appointment, supported by a caregiver. This is the purpose of the project.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_Journey}]
\progname{} presents one journey with one level for each procedure, and every
level is open at all times (\rref{FR_StartLevel}, \thmref{thm_Start}). The
supervisor recommended this design because the procedures needed at an
appointment can change at short notice.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_NoFail}]
\progname{} has no failure states. Mistakes lead to hints, and exercises can be
skipped without penalty (\rref{FR_HintOnMiss}, \rref{FR_NoPenalty}). This
follows from the goal of reducing anxiety.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_RewardOnce}]
Each level reward is given once, when the level is finished for the first time
(\thmref{thm_Once}). The contents of a reward may change (\lcref{LC_Rewards}),
but this rule will not.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_Offline}]
\progname{} works offline and sends no data off the device
(\conref{C_OfflineOperation}, \conref{C_NoNetworkCommunications}). Waiting
rooms may have poor connectivity, and nothing needs to be transmitted.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_NoPersonalInfo}]
\progname{} stores no personal or health information
(\conref{C_PatientPrivacy}, \nfrref{NFR_Minimization}). Changing this would
change the privacy and ethical basis of the project.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_Mobile}]
\progname{} runs on phones and tablets with touch screens, and it is built
with a cross-platform engine (\conref{C_MobilePlatforms},
\conref{C_CrossPlatformEngine}). Families already own these devices, and
dedicated hardware would defeat the goal of preparing at home.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_NoClaims}]
\progname{} is an educational preparation aid. It makes no diagnostic or
treatment claim (\nfrref{NFR_NoClaims}, \rref{FR_MedicalNotice}). A medical
function would make it a medical device, which is outside the scope of the
project.

\item[\ulcdef{ULC_NoRealMoney}]
\progname{} offers no purchases with real money and no advertising
(\rref{FR_NoRealMoney}, \conref{C_StoreCompliance}). Both would be
inappropriate for young children and conflict with app store policies for
children's applications.

\end{reqlist}
